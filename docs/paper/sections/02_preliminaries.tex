\section{Technical preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

This section fixes the field, the arguments and the chip model the rest of the paper builds on. Appendix~\ref{sec:app-prior} lists each result used from prior work and where this paper uses it.

\subsection{Field and multilinear extensions}
\label{sec:prelim-field}

\paragraph{Field.} All traces live over the prime field $\F = \KB{}$, $p = 2^{31} - 2^{24} + 1$, whose multiplicative group has two-adicity 24, which bounds the largest Reed--Solomon domain (\S\ref{sec:perf-proofsize}). Challenges and all sumcheck arithmetic use the extension $\EF = \F[X]/(X^4 - 3)$, so a sumcheck round of degree $d$ has soundness error $d/|\EF| \approx d \cdot 2^{-124}$. Since $2^{32} > p$, a 32-bit word is not one field element, and every word in the arithmetisation is four field elements holding its little-endian bytes.

\paragraph{Multilinear extensions and sumcheck.} For $f: \{0,1\}^n \to \F$ the multilinear extension is $\widetilde f(\bm z) = \sum_{\bm b} f(\bm b)\,\eqf(\bm b, \bm z)$ with $\eqf(\bm b, \bm z) = \prod_i (b_i z_i + (1-b_i)(1-z_i))$. The sumcheck protocol~\citep{lund1992algebraic} reduces a claim $\sum_{\bm b \in \{0,1\}^n} g(\bm b) = \sigma$ about a low-degree $g$ to a claim $g(\bm r) = \sigma'$ at a random $\bm r \in \EF^n$ in $n$ rounds of degree $\deg g$. We write a claim $\widetilde f(\bm z) = v$ as the pair $(\bm z, v)$.

\paragraph{Zerocheck.} To show $g(\bm b) = 0$ on a subcube, the verifier samples $\bm r$ and the prover runs a sumcheck on $\sum_{\bm b} \eqf(\bm b, \bm r)\, g(\bm b)$, which is zero for every $\bm r$ exactly when $g$ vanishes on the subcube, so a random $\bm r$ errs with probability at most the number of variables over $|\EF|$, plus the sumcheck's error. Section~\ref{sec:air-zerocheck} proves all constraints of a shard by one such check.

\subsection{Arguments and polynomial commitments}
\label{sec:prelim-args}

\begin{definition}[Argument of knowledge]
\label{def:snark}
Let $\mathcal R$ be a relation over public parameters $\mathsf{pp}$, a structure $s$ common to many instances, an instance $u$ and a witness $w$. An argument of knowledge for $\mathcal R$ is a tuple of algorithms $(\mathsf{Setup}, \mathsf{Prove}, \mathsf{Verify})$: $\mathsf{Setup}(\mathsf{pp}, s)$ outputs a proving key and a verifying key $(\mathsf{pk}, \mathsf{vk})$; $\mathsf{Prove}(\mathsf{pk}, u, w)$ outputs a proof $\pi$; and $\mathsf{Verify}(\mathsf{vk}, u, \pi)$ outputs a bit. It is \emph{complete} if an honest proof for $(\mathsf{pp}, s, u, w) \in \mathcal R$ always verifies, \emph{knowledge sound} with error $\varepsilon$ if for every efficient prover whose proof verifies with probability above $\varepsilon$ an extractor outputs a $w$ with $(\mathsf{pp}, s, u, w) \in \mathcal R$, and \emph{succinct} if the proof size and verification time are polylogarithmic in the size of the statement's computation.
\end{definition}

Here $s$ is a program image, $u$ the public values of an execution and $w$ its input and hint streams (\S\ref{sec:statement}); the construction provides no zero knowledge.

\begin{definition}[Multilinear polynomial commitment]
\label{def:pcs}
A polynomial commitment scheme for multilinear polynomials is a tuple $(\mathsf{Commit}, \mathsf{Open}, \mathsf{VerifyOpen})$. $\mathsf{Commit}(\mathsf{pp}, f)$ outputs a commitment $C$ to an $m$-variate multilinear $f$ and a prover state; $\mathsf{Open}$ is an interactive protocol, compiled by Fiat--Shamir, in which the prover convinces the verifier that $f(\bm z) = v$ for a given point $\bm z \in \EF^{m}$ and value $v$, and $\mathsf{VerifyOpen}(C, \bm z, v, \pi) \in \{0,1\}$ is its verifier. It is \emph{binding} if no efficient prover opens one commitment to two different polynomials, and \emph{knowledge sound} if an accepting opening yields an $f$ consistent with $C$ and $f(\bm z) = v$.
\end{definition}

A polynomial interactive oracle proof, in which the verifier only evaluates the prover's polynomials at a few points, compiled with a polynomial commitment gives a succinct argument~\citep{bcs16}; every argument of this paper is of that kind.

\paragraph{Reed--Solomon and constrained Reed--Solomon codes.} For a smooth domain $L \subset \F^{*}$ (a coset of a subgroup of order $2^{d}$) the code $\RS[\F, L, m]$ is the set of functions $L \to \F$ that agree with a polynomial of degree $< 2^m$; its rate is $\rho = 2^m/|L|$. A codeword is also the evaluation of an $m$-variate multilinear $\widehat f$ at the points $(x, x^2, x^4, \dots, x^{2^{m-1}})$, $x \in L$. Following Arnon et al.~\citep{whir}, the \emph{constrained} code $\CRS[\F, L, m, \widehat w, \sigma]$ is the subset of $\RS[\F, L, m]$ whose multilinear satisfies $\sum_{\bm b \in \{0,1\}^m} \widehat w(\widehat f(\bm b), \bm b) = \sigma$; the weight $\widehat w(Z, \bm X) = Z \cdot \eqf(\bm X, \bm z)$ expresses the claim $\widehat f(\bm z) = \sigma$. A \emph{fold} by a challenge $\alpha$ maps $f: L \to \F$ to $\Fold(f, \alpha)(x^2) = \tfrac{f(x) + f(-x)}{2} + \alpha \tfrac{f(x) - f(-x)}{2x}$, a codeword of $\RS[\F, L^{(2)}, m-1]$ whenever $f$ is a codeword, equal to the partial evaluation of $\widehat f$ at $X_1 = \alpha$.

\paragraph{Hashing and transcripts.} Merkle commitments and the Fiat--Shamir transcript~\citep{fiat-shamir} use a hash function $\Hash$ with a digest of $d$ bits, of which the argument requires collision resistance for the commitments and the random-oracle model for the transcript; nothing else in the protocol depends on which function it is. Two instantiations fit. The $\Poseidon{}$ permutation~\citep{poseidon2} over $\F$ is algebraic, so a recursive verifier evaluates it in few constraints, and it costs more to compute on the prover. BLAKE3~\citep{blake3} is a byte-oriented hash that is faster to compute and costs more constraints inside a recursive verifier. Only the first is implemented and measured: every commitment, transcript, key and timing in this paper uses $\Poseidon{}$ at width 16 with $d = 248$. Its S-box has degree three, so the schedule is eight full and twenty partial rounds; the thirteen partial rounds tabulated for a degree-seven S-box give less margin, and the measured configuration of \S\ref{sec:perf-revision} carried that count. The same permutation serves the transcript, commitment trees and recursion machine. Grinding $b$ bits means searching for a nonce whose absorption yields $b$ leading zero bits in the squeezed challenge; it multiplies the cost of the corresponding cheating strategy by $2^{b}$.

\subsection{Lookup arguments and memory checking}
\label{sec:prelim-lookups}

\paragraph{Lookups as fractions.} A lookup argument proves that two multisets of tuples agree. In the logarithmic-derivative form of Hab\"ock and its GKR realisation~\citep{haboeck,logup-gkr}, written \LogUp{}, a tuple $\bm f$ on bus $\mathsf{bus}$ with multiplicity $m$ contributes $m/(\alpha - \mathsf{bus} - \sum_{j \ge 1} \beta^{j} f_j)$ for random $\alpha, \beta \in \EF$; senders and receivers must sum to the same value, which a layered GKR circuit~\citep{gkr-cmt} computes and proves by sumcheck (\S\ref{sec:air-logup}).

\paragraph{Uses of the lookup argument.} The buses ask this argument for three things, named by the distinctions introduced with Lasso~\citep{lasso} and used by Jolt~\citep{jolt}. An \emph{indexed} lookup proves $a_i = T[b_i]$ for committed indices $b$; an \emph{unindexed} lookup proves only that each $a_i$ occurs in $T$, as a range check does. An indexed lookup is a read from a read-only memory~\citep{twist-shout}, so the program bus and the byte bus are indexed lookups into preprocessed tables, and range checks are unindexed. Registers and memory are read--write, where a lookup no longer suffices, since a read could be answered with a value written only later.

\begin{definition}[Memory checking~{\citep[\S2.2]{twist-shout}}]
\label{def:memcheck}
Consider a memory of $K$ cells, an initial content $\mathit{init} \in \F^{K}$ and $T$ accesses in time order, access $j$ carrying an address $a_j$, a returned value $v_j$ and a written value $v'_j$ (for a read, $v'_j = v_j$). The accesses are \emph{consistent} if every $v_j$ equals $v'_{j'}$ for the largest $j' < j$ with $a_{j'} = a_j$, or $\mathit{init}[a_j]$ if there is none. A \emph{memory-checking argument} proves consistency of committed accesses; with no writes and public contents it is an indexed lookup.
\end{definition}

Memory-checking arguments either reorder the trace by address or do not~\citep[App.~A]{twist-shout}. \Ziren{}'s does not: it is Blum et al.'s offline check~\citep{blum1994} with Spice's timestamps~\citep{spice}, which makes consistency the balance of one multiset relation plus a range fact per access (\S\ref{sec:air-memory}, Lemma~\ref{lem:memory}). The timestamp is the frame's $(\mathsf{shard}, \mathsf{clk}+\mathsf{pos})$, already ordered by the state bus, so one range-checked difference suffices where Spice's rule needs two. The fingerprinting error grows linearly in the number of accesses and cells~\citep[\S2.7]{twist-shout}, which over $\EF$ is negligible.

\subsection{Chips, buses and determinism}
\label{sec:prelim-chips}

\Ziren{}'s constraint system is a set of \emph{chips}, tables whose rows satisfy local polynomial constraints and which communicate only through buses.

\begin{definition}[Chip]
\label{def:chip}
A \emph{chip} is a table over $\F$ of a fixed width $w$ together with a set of polynomial constraints of degree at most three in the $w$ columns of one row, a distinguished boolean column $\mathsf{is\_real}$ gating every constraint, and a set of \emph{interactions}. A row is \emph{satisfying} if it satisfies every constraint.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interaction and bus]
\label{def:bus}
An interaction of a chip is a triple $(\mathsf{bus}, \bm f, m)$ where $\mathsf{bus}$ is a name, $\bm f$ is a tuple of linear expressions in the row's columns and $m$ is a linear expression in the columns (the multiplicity), marked as a \emph{send} or a \emph{receive}. Multiplicities are non-negative integers bounded well below $p$: senders use zero, one, or a value range-checked against the byte table, and the preprocessed \texttt{Program}, \texttt{Byte} and \texttt{Range} tables receive with use counts that the shard's cycle budget bounds by $2^{25}$. For a set of tables, the \emph{multiset of a bus} is $\{(\bm f(r))\}$ with multiplicity $\sum m(r)$ over the sends of all rows $r$ of all chips, and likewise for the receives. A bus \emph{balances} if its send and receive multisets are equal.
\end{definition}

Two facts connect balance to the fraction identity over $\EF$. The multiplicity bound stops the counters wrapping modulo $p$, so balance is a statement about integer multiplicities; the exact condition is a positive total below $p$ and a negative total above $-p$~\citep[Cor.~3.6]{openvm-logup}. And the identity is checked after compressing tuples with sampled challenges, so an unbalanced bus can still satisfy it, with probability bounded by Schwartz--Zippel; Section~\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} charges that error.

\begin{definition}[Shard relation]
\label{def:shard}
A \emph{shard} is a set of tables, one per chip, together with public values $\mathsf{pv} = (\mathsf{sh}, \mathsf{entry}, \mathsf{exit}, \mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{in}}, \mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{out}}, D, h, \mathsf{code})$: its shard index; its entry and exit states, each a program-counter pair with the clock; the address cursors of the global memory tables before and after it; its cross-shard digest, a point of an elliptic curve over the degree-7 extension $\F_{p^7}$ (\S\ref{sec:air-global}); the digest of committed output; and the exit code. It is \emph{valid} for a verifying key $\mathsf{vk}$ if its preprocessed tables are those $\mathsf{vk}$ commits to, every real row is satisfying and every bus balances, where the public-values table contributes the entry state as a send and the exit state as a receive on the state bus, and the program bus is received by the preprocessed program table with the recorded multiplicities.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Inputs, outputs and determinism of a chip]
\label{def:det}
For a chip $U$, the \emph{inputs} $I_U(r)$ of a row $r$ are the values the row consumes from elsewhere: the tuples of its receives, the program tuple it fetches, the previous-value part of its memory accesses, and its \emph{declared free values}, the values its constraints leave to the prover by design, which the statements of \S\ref{sec:verification-det} list. The \emph{outputs} $O_U(r)$ are the values it produces for elsewhere: the tuples of its sends other than the fetch, and the new-value part of its memory accesses. Fetch and previous value are sends in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:bus}, but inputs of the row, so the direction of an interaction is not the direction of the data. $U$ is \emph{deterministic} if for all satisfying real rows $r, r'$,
\[
  I_U(r) = I_U(r') \;\Rightarrow\; O_U(r) = O_U(r').
\]
This is the notion of Picus~\citep{picus} adapted to the interaction language.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Guest, host and cost metrics.} The \emph{guest} is the MIPS32 program and its runtime, whose execution is proved; \emph{guest cycles} count its executed instructions. The \emph{host} runs the executor and the prover, and the verifier trusts nothing it computes. The \emph{trace area} of a shard or block is the sum over chips of rows $\times$ columns of the main trace, in cells; the \emph{(row, interaction) count} is the number of bus interactions over all rows, the leaf count of the lookup circuit; and \emph{throughput} is proved guest cycles per second of wall-clock time. Every committed value lies in the base field and costs one cell whatever its width, so instruction efficiency is improved by removing cells, not narrowing them (\S\ref{sec:intro-costs}).
