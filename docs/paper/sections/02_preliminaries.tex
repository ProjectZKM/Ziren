\section{Technical preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

This section fixes the field, the arguments and the chip model; Appendix~\ref{sec:app-prior} lists the results imported from prior work.

\subsection{Field, sumcheck and commitments}
\label{sec:prelim-field}
\label{sec:prelim-args}

\paragraph{Field.} All traces live over the prime field $\F = \KB{}$, $p = 2^{31} - 2^{24} + 1$, whose multiplicative group has two-adicity 24, which bounds the largest Reed--Solomon domain (\S\ref{sec:perf-proofsize}). Challenges and all sumcheck arithmetic use the extension $\EF = \F[X]/(X^5 + X^2 - 1)$, so a sumcheck round of degree $d$ has soundness error $d/|\EF| \approx d \cdot 2^{-155}$. Since $2^{32} > p$, a 32-bit word is not one field element, and every word in the arithmetisation is four field elements holding its little-endian bytes.

\paragraph{Multilinear extensions, sumcheck and zerocheck.} For $f: \{0,1\}^n \to \F$ the multilinear extension is $\widetilde f(\bm z) = \sum_{\bm b} f(\bm b)\,\eqf(\bm b, \bm z)$ with $\eqf(\bm b, \bm z) = \prod_i (b_i z_i + (1-b_i)(1-z_i))$; we write a claim $\widetilde f(\bm z) = v$ as the pair $(\bm z, v)$. The sumcheck protocol~\citep{lund1992algebraic} reduces a claim $\sum_{\bm b \in \{0,1\}^n} g(\bm b) = \sigma$ about a low-degree $g$ to a claim $g(\bm r) = \sigma'$ at a random $\bm r \in \EF^n$ in $n$ rounds of degree $\deg g$. To show that $g$ vanishes on a subcube, a \emph{zerocheck} samples $\bm r$ and runs a sumcheck on $\sum_{\bm b} \eqf(\bm b, \bm r)\, g(\bm b)$, which is zero for every $\bm r$ exactly when $g$ vanishes there; a random $\bm r$ errs with probability at most the number of variables over $|\EF|$, plus the sumcheck's error (\S\ref{sec:air-zerocheck}).

\paragraph{Arguments and commitments.} An \emph{argument of knowledge} for a relation $\mathcal R$ over a structure $s$, an instance $u$ and a witness $w$ is a triple $(\mathsf{Setup}, \mathsf{Prove}, \mathsf{Verify})$ that is complete and \emph{knowledge sound} with error $\varepsilon$: from every efficient prover whose proof verifies with probability above $\varepsilon$, an extractor outputs a witness. Here $s$ is a program image, $u$ the public values of an execution and $w$ its input and hint streams (\S\ref{sec:statement}). The construction up to the compressed proof provides no zero knowledge; the optional terminal proof of \S\ref{sec:zk} adds it. A \emph{multilinear polynomial commitment} lets the prover commit to an $m$-variate multilinear $f$ and later prove $f(\bm z) = v$ at a point $\bm z \in \EF^{m}$, and is \emph{binding} if no efficient prover opens one commitment to two different polynomials. A polynomial interactive oracle proof, in which the verifier only evaluates the prover's polynomials at a few points, compiled with such a commitment gives a succinct argument~\citep{bcs16}; every argument of this paper is of that kind.

\paragraph{Reed--Solomon and constrained Reed--Solomon codes.} For a smooth domain $L \subset \F^{*}$ (a coset of a subgroup of order $2^{d}$) the code $\RS[\F, L, m]$ is the set of functions $L \to \F$ that agree with a polynomial of degree $< 2^m$; its rate is $\rho = 2^m/|L|$. A codeword is also the evaluation of an $m$-variate multilinear $\widehat f$ at the points $(x, x^2, x^4, \dots, x^{2^{m-1}})$, $x \in L$. Following Arnon et al.~\citep{whir}, the \emph{constrained} code $\CRS[\F, L, m, \widehat w, \sigma]$ is the subset of $\RS[\F, L, m]$ whose multilinear satisfies $\sum_{\bm b \in \{0,1\}^m} \widehat w(\widehat f(\bm b), \bm b) = \sigma$; the weight $\widehat w(Z, \bm X) = Z \cdot \eqf(\bm X, \bm z)$ expresses the claim $\widehat f(\bm z) = \sigma$. A \emph{fold} by a challenge $\alpha$ maps a codeword $f$ to $\Fold(f, \alpha)(x^2) = \tfrac{f(x) + f(-x)}{2} + \alpha \tfrac{f(x) - f(-x)}{2x}$, a codeword of $\RS[\F, L^{(2)}, m-1]$ equal to the partial evaluation of $\widehat f$ at $X_1 = \alpha$.

\paragraph{Hashing and transcripts.} Merkle commitments and the Fiat--Shamir transcript~\citep{fiat-shamir} use a hash function $\Hash$ with a digest of $d$ bits. The argument assumes collision resistance for the commitments and models the transcript hash as a random oracle. Every commitment, transcript, key and timing in this paper uses the $\Poseidon{}$ permutation~\citep{poseidon2} over $\F$ at width 16 with $d = 248$, which a recursive verifier evaluates in few constraints; its S-box has degree three, so the schedule is eight full and twenty partial rounds. Grinding $b$ bits means searching for a nonce whose absorption yields $b$ leading zero bits in the squeezed challenge; it multiplies the cost of the corresponding cheating strategy by $2^{b}$.

\subsection{Lookup arguments and memory checking}
\label{sec:prelim-lookups}

A lookup argument proves that two multisets of tuples agree. In the logarithmic-derivative form of Hab\"ock and its GKR realisation~\citep{haboeck,logup-gkr}, written \LogUp{}, a tuple $\bm f$ on bus $\mathsf{bus}$ with multiplicity $m$ contributes $m/(\alpha - \mathsf{bus} - \sum_{j \ge 1} \beta^{j} f_j)$ for random $\alpha, \beta \in \EF$; senders and receivers must sum to the same value, which a layered GKR circuit~\citep{gkr-cmt} computes and proves by sumcheck (\S\ref{sec:air-logup}).

In the terms of Lasso~\citep{lasso}, the program bus and the byte bus are \emph{indexed} lookups, which prove $a_i = T[b_i]$ for committed indices $b$ and so read a read-only memory~\citep{twist-shout}, and range checks are \emph{unindexed}, proving only that each $a_i$ occurs in $T$. Registers and memory are read--write, where a lookup no longer suffices, since a read could be answered with a value written only later.

\begin{definition}[Memory checking~{\citep[\S2.2]{twist-shout}}]
\label{def:memcheck}
Consider a memory of $K$ cells, an initial content $\mathit{init} \in \F^{K}$ and $T$ accesses in time order, access $j$ carrying an address $a_j$, a returned value $v_j$ and a written value $v'_j$ (for a read, $v'_j = v_j$). The accesses are \emph{consistent} if every $v_j$ equals $v'_{j'}$ for the largest $j' < j$ with $a_{j'} = a_j$, or $\mathit{init}[a_j]$ if there is none. A \emph{memory-checking argument} proves consistency of committed accesses; with no writes and public contents it is an indexed lookup.
\end{definition}

\Ziren{}'s memory-checking argument does not reorder the trace by address~\citep[App.~A]{twist-shout}: it is Blum et al.'s offline check~\citep{blum1994} with Spice's timestamps~\citep{spice}, which makes consistency the balance of one multiset relation plus a range fact per access (Lemma~\ref{lem:memory}). Its fingerprinting error grows linearly in the number of accesses and cells~\citep[\S2.7]{twist-shout} and is included in the bounds of \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness}.

\subsection{Chips, buses and determinism}
\label{sec:prelim-chips}

\begin{definition}[Chip]
\label{def:chip}
A \emph{chip} is a table over $\F$ of a fixed width $w$ together with a set of polynomial constraints of degree at most three in the $w$ columns of one row, a distinguished boolean \emph{gating column}, the real-row flag, which gates every constraint and is set exactly on the \emph{real} rows, and a set of \emph{interactions}. A row is \emph{satisfying} if it satisfies every constraint.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interaction and bus]
\label{def:bus}
An interaction of a chip is a triple $(\mathsf{bus}, \bm f, m)$ where $\mathsf{bus}$ is a name, $\bm f$ is a tuple of linear expressions in the row's columns and $m$ is a linear expression in the columns (the multiplicity), marked as a \emph{send} or a \emph{receive}. Multiplicities are non-negative integers bounded well below $p$: senders use zero, one, or a value range-checked against the byte table, and the preprocessed program, byte and range tables receive with use counts that the shard's cycle budget bounds by $2^{25}$. For a set of tables, the \emph{multiset of a bus} is $\{(\bm f(r))\}$ with multiplicity $\sum m(r)$ over the sends of all rows $r$ of all chips, and likewise for the receives. A bus \emph{balances} if its send and receive multisets are equal.
\end{definition}

The multiplicity bound stops the counters wrapping modulo $p$, so balance is a statement about integer multiplicities; the exact condition is a positive total below $p$ and a negative total above $-p$~\citep[Cor.~3.6]{openvm-logup}. The fraction identity over $\EF$ is checked after compressing tuples with sampled challenges, so an unbalanced bus can still satisfy it, with probability bounded by Schwartz--Zippel; Section~\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} charges that error.

\begin{definition}[Shard relation]
\label{def:shard}
A \emph{shard} is a set of tables, one per chip, together with public values $\mathsf{pv} = (\mathsf{sh}, \mathsf{entry}, \mathsf{exit}, \mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{in}}, \mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{out}}, S, h, \mathsf{code})$: its shard index; its entry and exit states, each a program-counter pair with the clock; the address cursors of the global memory tables before and after it; the sum $S \in \EF$ of its global fractions under a global challenge $(\alpha_g, \beta_g)$ (\S\ref{sec:air-global}); the digest of committed output; and the exit code. \emph{Global} interactions are those whose two sides may lie in different shards; they balance only over the execution. A shard is \emph{valid} for a verifying key $\mathsf{vk}$ and a global challenge if its preprocessed tables are those $\mathsf{vk}$ commits to, every real row is satisfying, every bus balances on the shard's interactions other than the global ones, and the fractions of its global interactions under the global challenge sum to $S$; the public-values table contributes the entry state as a send and the exit state as a receive on the state bus, and the program bus is received by the preprocessed program table with the recorded multiplicities.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Inputs, outputs and determinism of a chip]
\label{def:det}
For a chip $U$, the \emph{inputs} $I_U(r)$ of a row $r$ are the values the row consumes from elsewhere: the tuples of its receives, the program tuple it fetches, the previous-value part of its memory accesses, and its \emph{declared free values}, the values its constraints leave to the prover by design, which the statements of \S\ref{sec:verification-det} list. The \emph{outputs} $O_U(r)$ are the values it produces for elsewhere: the tuples of its sends other than the fetch, and the new-value part of its memory accesses. Fetch and previous value are sends in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:bus}, but inputs of the row, so the direction of an interaction is not the direction of the data. $U$ is \emph{deterministic} if for all satisfying real rows $r, r'$,
\[
  I_U(r) = I_U(r') \;\Rightarrow\; O_U(r) = O_U(r').
\]
This is the notion of Picus~\citep{picus} adapted to the interaction language.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Guest, host and cost metrics.} The \emph{guest} is the MIPS32 program and its runtime, whose execution is proved; \emph{guest cycles} count its executed instructions. The \emph{host} runs the executor and the prover, and the verifier trusts nothing it computes. The \emph{trace area} of a shard or block is the sum over chips of rows $\times$ columns of the main trace, in cells; the \emph{(row, interaction) count} is the number of bus interactions over all rows, the leaf count of the lookup circuit; and \emph{throughput} is proved guest cycles per second of wall-clock time (\S\ref{sec:intro-costs}).
