\subsection{Chips for the base instruction set}
\label{sec:base}

Each instruction family is proved by a chip, in a register and an immediate form where the family has both, made of an instruction frame (\S\ref{sec:air-frame}) and columns of its own that fix the result from the operand words. Operands follow Definition~\ref{def:format}: $a$ is the word written to (or, for stores and branches, read from) register $\mathsf{op\_a}$, and $b$ and $c$ are the words of $\mathsf{op\_b}$ and $\mathsf{op\_c}$, from registers or decoded immediates, in the notation of \S\ref{sec:air-chips}. Every result constraint is switched off when the destination is register 0, whose written word the frame pins to zero. We work addition and control transfer in full and summarise the other families; Table~\ref{tab:chips} gives the frame, width and added interactions of every chip.

\paragraph{Frames.} The frame's interactions are paid on every row: 20 for the R-type frame and 16 for the I-type and shift-amount frames, itemised in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-frame}. The \emph{universal} frame is the R-type frame with its reads gated by the immediate flags. The four frames play the part of OpenVM's six RV32IM adapters~\citep{openvm}; loads, stores and branches share the I-type frame. Interactions are counted below beyond the frame's.

\paragraph{Addition and subtraction.}\label{sec:base-arith} Addition is checked byte by byte with carries that are not columns: the carry out of byte $i$ is the linear expression $\theta_i = (b_i + c_i - a_i + \theta_{i-1})/256$, $\theta_{-1} = 0$, and the chip asserts $\theta_i(\theta_i - 1) = 0$; subtraction asserts the same of $a + c = b$. The frame byte-checks the result, so the addition chip adds four columns (two selectors, two gates) and no lookup to its frame. Jolt instead forms $b + c$ as one element of a 256-bit field and looks up its low bits~\citep{jolt}, which $2^{32} > p$ rules out here. \texttt{lui} adds an immediate shifted at decode to register 0, the moves to and from \texttt{HI} and \texttt{LO} add zero, and \texttt{add} and \texttt{addi} decode as \texttt{addu} and \texttt{addiu} (\S\ref{sec:isa-emulator}).

\paragraph{Bitwise operations, comparisons and shifts.}\label{sec:base-shift} These families take their byte-level facts from the byte table (\S\ref{sec:air-bytes}). A bitwise result is fixed by lookups alone, one per byte pair, so the bitwise chip adds five columns and four lookups. A comparison is Jolt's signed less-than~\citep{jolt}, $a = x_s(1-y_s) + \mathsf{eq}(x_s, y_s)\,\mathsf{LTU}(x_{<s}, y_{<s})$ with $x = b$ and $y = c$, the unsigned forms comparing the full words: two lookups split off the sign bits, a witnessed one-hot flag marks the most significant differing byte, and one unsigned less-than lookup decides the flagged pair. A shift by $k = 8q + r$, with $k$ the low five bits of the amount, is a shift by $r$ bits followed by a relabelling of whole bytes by $q$. A left shift multiplies by $2^{r}$ with witnessed per-byte carries. A right shift first extends $b$ to eight bytes, whose upper four are zero for a logical shift, the sign fill for an arithmetic shift and a copy of $b$ for a rotation, so one right shift leaves the rotation in the low half; its bit part is eight lookups that map a byte and a 3-bit amount to the shifted byte and the bits shifted out, Jolt's shift decomposition with the byte part done by relabelling.

\paragraph{Branches and jumps.}\label{sec:base-control} A transfer sets only $\mathsf{next\_next\_pc}$, the second component of the pair it sends on the state bus; its delay-slot instruction is the next row of the chain, an ordinary row (Example~\ref{ex:delay}). The branch chip compares $a$ (register $\mathit{rs}$) with $b$ (register $\mathit{rt}$, or register 0 for the compare-with-zero forms). Equality is two is-zero tests, by witnessed inverses, on the differences of the 16-bit halves; the sign of $a$ is one sign-bit lookup, gated to the compare-with-zero forms; and $a > 0$ is $(1 - a_s)(1 - [a = 0])$. The offset is shifted and sign-extended at decode, so the taken target $\mathsf{next\_pc} + \mathit{off}$ is one in-row addition with a byte-checked result, and one unsigned less-than lookup per counter word proves it canonical. The jump chip writes the link $\mathsf{next\_pc} + 4$, which is $\mathsf{pc} + 8$ through the chain, and takes its target from the immediate, from register $b$ or, for \texttt{bal}, from an addition; \texttt{j} and \texttt{jal} take the region bits as zero (\S\ref{sec:isa-emulator}).

\paragraph{Loads and stores.}\label{sec:base-memory} Five chips, for word loads, word stores, narrow loads, narrow stores and unaligned accesses, share one block of columns and nine interactions. The effective address $b + \mathrm{sext}(\mathit{off})$ is one in-row addition with range-checked result bytes; lookups prove it canonical and above the 36 register slots, and extract its two low bits, from which the aligned address is linear. The access is one word-granular access at sub-cycle position 0 (two memory-bus interactions and two timestamp lookups), and the word is not byte-checked again (\S\ref{sec:air-memory}). The word forms assert that the low bits are zero; the narrow and unaligned forms turn them into one-hot offset flags and select or merge bytes polynomially. OpenVM likewise accesses aligned four-cell blocks and selects bytes within the row~\citep[\S3.2.4]{openvm}, where the original Jolt design spends one memory-checking operation per byte~\citep{jolt}; the price is the offset flags and merge columns that make the narrow and unaligned chips the widest of the family.
