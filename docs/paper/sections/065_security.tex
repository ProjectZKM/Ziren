\section{Security}
\label{sec:security}

This section states the relation the block proof is an argument for, accounts for the soundness of each proof, and composes those levels into a statement about the non-interactive block proof (\S\S\ref{sec:security-relation}--\ref{sec:iopp-layers}). It then adds an optional zero-knowledge terminal proof (\S\ref{sec:zk}) and treats what ties the block proof to the MIPS32 machine: key binding, the global challenge, trace uniqueness, conformance and chip determinism (\S\S\ref{sec:security-binding}--\ref{sec:verification}), and quantum adversaries (\S\ref{sec:pq}).

\subsection{The proved relation}
\label{sec:security-relation}

The shard argument proves one shard; the statement of Section~\ref{sec:statement} is about an execution. Between the two sits the arithmetised relation the recursion tree is knowledge sound for, with each condition assigned to the component that enforces it:
\begin{align*}
  \mathcal R_{\mathrm{arith}} = \Big\{ (\mathsf{vk}, h) :\ \exists\, g^\star, w, (\mathsf{T}_i, \mathsf{pv}_i)_{i<N}\
  & \forall i:\ \mathsf{T}_i \text{ is a valid shard for } \mathsf{pv}_i,\ \mathsf{vk}
    && \text{(shard and global proofs)} \\
  & \phantom{\forall i:\ }\text{and } \mathsf{chal}(g^\star, w),\ h_i = h \\
  \wedge\ & \forall i:\ g_{\mathrm{out},i} = \mathsf{step}(g_{\mathrm{in},i}, \mathsf{T}_i),
    && \text{(shard and global leaves)} \\
  & \phantom{\forall i:\ }(g^\star_i, w_i) = (g^\star, w),\ \mathsf{pow}(g^\star, w) \\
  \wedge\ & \forall i<N-1:\ \mathsf{sh}_{i+1} = \mathsf{sh}_i + 1,\ \mathsf{exit}_i = \mathsf{entry}_{i+1},
    && \text{(compose nodes)} \\
  & \phantom{\forall i<N-1:\ }\mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{out},i} = \mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{in},i+1},\ g_{\mathrm{out},i} = g_{\mathrm{in},i+1} \\
  \wedge\ & \mathsf{entry}_0 = \mathsf{start}(\mathsf{vk}),\ \mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{in},0} = 0,\ g_{\mathrm{in},0} = g_0(\mathsf{vk})
    && \text{(first-shard leaf)} \\
  \wedge\ & \mathsf{sh}_0 = 1,\ \mathsf{pc}(\mathsf{exit}_{N-1}) = 0,\ g_{\mathrm{out},N-1} = g^\star,
    && \text{(root)} \\
  & \textstyle\sum_i S_i = 0,\ \forall i:\ \mathsf{code}_i = 0 \Big\}
\end{align*}
Validity is Definition~\ref{def:shard}; $\mathsf{sh}_i$, $h_i$ and $\mathsf{code}_i$ are fields of $\mathsf{pv}_i$; the halt row sets the exit program counter to zero; $\mathsf{start}(\mathsf{vk})$ is the start state the key fixes; the entry and exit states are program-counter pairs; the cursors tile the address space. For the cross-shard interactions (\S\ref{sec:air-global}), $g_{\mathrm{in},i}$ and $g_{\mathrm{out},i}$ are the global chain before and after shard $i$, $g^\star_i$ is the chain end its global proof was proved under, $w_i$ the proof-of-work witness of that proof's global challenge and $S_i \in \EF$ the sum of its global fractions, zero for a shard without a global round; $g_0(\mathsf{vk})$ is the chain start the key fixes, $\mathsf{step}$ extends the chain by the shard's global round, $\mathsf{pow}(g^\star, w)$ holds when $w$ passes the proof of work of \S\ref{sec:global-security}, and $\mathsf{chal}(g^\star, w)$ is the global challenge $(\alpha_g, \beta_g)$ derived from them. Two proofs establish the validity of a shard: the shard proof every clause of Definition~\ref{def:shard} but the last, and the shard's global proof the sum $S_i$. That both speak of the same tables is argued in \S\ref{sec:global-security}.

Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} is knowledge soundness for $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arith}}$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:unique}, with Lemma~\ref{lem:xmem} joining the shards' memory into one execution and the conformance evidence of \S\ref{sec:verification}, connects $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arith}}$ to the relation $\mathcal R$ of Section~\ref{sec:statement}. Neither theorem depends on a particular chip set, field or proximity test.

\paragraph{Open hypotheses.} The end-to-end statement holds under four hypotheses that this paper does not discharge:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item round-by-round knowledge soundness of every node's committed protocol, which carries function binding of the jagged \WHIR{} commitment with an unquantified loss (\S\ref{sec:iopp-layers});
  \item that each node's extractor succeeds on the transcripts its parent's extractor produces (\S\ref{sec:iopp-layers});
  \item that the leaf and compose programs, which the determinism proofs do not cover, enforce the compose relation, the key binding included (\S\ref{sec:security-binding});
  \item determinism of the two SHA-256 control chips, well-formed shard boundary rows (one per touched word, linked to actual accesses), and real-row exhaustiveness, the open premises of the memory and trace-uniqueness arguments (\S\ref{sec:air-unique}).
\end{enumerate}
Conformance evidence (\S\ref{sec:verification}), not a proof, identifies the arithmetised machine with the MIPS32 instruction set.

\subsection{Soundness accounting}
\label{sec:iopp-soundness}

\paragraph{Method.} We evaluate the component errors of the interactive protocol with the soundcalc calculator for hash-based proof systems~\citep{soundcalc}, extended to jagged commitments over \WHIR{}, for the schedule of Table~\ref{tab:whir-schedule}. The components are the out-of-domain samples and shift queries of each \WHIR{} round, the batching of the committed stripes in the opening (column batching is counted in the jagged reduction), each folding round, the final polynomial, the three terms of the jagged reduction (Section~\ref{sec:pcs}), the zerocheck and the \LogUp{} argument, with grinding counted inside the term it protects and the final query phase counted once. Query soundness is counted in the unique-decoding regime for the first oracle of the core schedule and under the Johnson bound, with multiplicity $m = 3$, for its later oracles and for every oracle of the compress schedule~\citep{proximity-gaps-2025}; neither needs a proximity-gap conjecture. Where the first oracle is counted under the Johnson bound, the arguments above the opening are charged the list size of that oracle. A proof's level is $-\log_2$ of the \emph{sum} of its component errors, a union bound rather than the smallest component, and a block's level sums over every node of its recursion tree. The analysis follows \WHIR{}'s~\citep{whir} with the batching over several commitments and the jagged terms added; levels from other calculators~\citep{risc0-soundness} are comparable only when they charge the same components.

\paragraph{Scope of the zerocheck figures.} Table~\ref{tab:components} is computed for the binary zerocheck. The univariate refinement of \S\ref{sec:air-skip} replaces the zerocheck's $4n/|\EF|$ sumcheck term by $(4n+44)/|\EF|$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:skip-round-error}), an additional $44/|\EF|$ per proof using the skip, which must be included before a stage or recursion-tree bound is recomputed for that configuration.

\paragraph{Result.} Every component of both schedules has at least 128 bits of interactive soundness, the level the schedules are solved for, and each stage 124 bits by the union bound (Table~\ref{tab:components}); the union bound for one core-schedule proof and one compress-schedule proof gives 123.12 bits. The folding rounds are the largest error group in both schedules, because each of their challenges past the first round of the core schedule is ground to the target and no further (Table~\ref{tab:whir-schedule}). We call $-\log_2 \sum_i \varepsilon_i$, summed over every node $i$ of a block's recursion tree, the block's \emph{composite security}; when all nodes have the same error bound, this is $\log_2$ of the node count below that common per-proof level, and for different schedules we sum their individual errors. For an illustrative tree of 150 proofs, comprising 149 proofs under the core schedule and one root under the compress schedule, it is 116.94 bits. These figures bound the interactive protocol's information-theoretic terms.

The global challenge adds one term per block rather than per proof, the $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{glob}}$ of \S\ref{sec:global-security}: about $2^{-146.6}$ for the block measured there, with its 22 bits of grinding, which leaves the composite figure of the illustrative tree at 116.94 bits. Applying the $Q$ factor of Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} to these quantified interactive terms gives $116.94 - \log_2 Q$ bits, or 76.9 bits at $Q = 2^{40}$. This calculation excludes the unquantified function-binding loss of Layer~2 (\S\ref{sec:iopp-layers}) and the theorem's separate hash-collision term. It therefore does not give a numerical bound on the full non-interactive proof's security.

\subsection{Security in three layers}
\label{sec:iopp-layers}

The figures above bound an interactive protocol; the published proof is its Fiat--Shamir compilation. Following the commit-and-open paradigm in its functional form~\citep{funky26}, we separate the argument into three layers, each with its own security statement.

\paragraph{Layer 1: a functional interactive oracle proof.} A \emph{query class} is a set $\mathcal Q$ of functions $\alpha : \Sigma^{\ell} \to D$ on strings over an alphabet $\Sigma$. A public-coin \emph{functional interactive oracle proof} (FIOP) with query class $\mathcal Q$~\citep{funky26} is an interactive oracle proof whose verifier accesses each prover message $\Pi_i$ only through constraints $(\alpha, \alpha(\Pi_i))$ with $\alpha \in \mathcal Q$; it is knowledge sound with error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FIOP}}$ if an extractor, given one accepting transcript, outputs a witness except with probability $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FIOP}}$.

The shard protocol of Section~\ref{sec:argument} is an FIOP for the \emph{evaluation} query class over $\Sigma = \F$: a committed table $\Pi \in \F^{2^n}$ is read only through $\alpha_{\bm z}(\Pi) = \widetilde\Pi(\bm z) \in \EF$ at points $\bm z \in \EF^{n}$, and a message the verifier reads in full, such as a sumcheck round polynomial, is a short string queried at every position. In Table~\ref{tab:components}, the rows for \LogUp{}, the zerocheck and the reduction to the dense polynomial bound $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FIOP}}$, and the rows for query phases, folding rounds, the final polynomial and the out-of-domain samples belong to the opening of Layer~2; batching sits at the boundary, since it combines claims just before they are opened. Under the univariate refinement the column query is the weighted linear functional of Equation~\eqref{eq:skip-row-opening} rather than a row-MLE evaluation; Section~\ref{sec:pcs-skip} reduces it to the same dense-polynomial evaluation query, and a function-binding statement for that instantiation must cover these modified column queries.

\paragraph{Layer 2: the interactive commit-and-open argument.} Replacing each oracle by a commitment under a functional commitment scheme for the same query class, and opening every queried constraint after the last round, gives an interactive argument~\citep{funky26}; for \Ziren{} the commitment is the jagged commitment of Section~\ref{sec:pcs} opened through \WHIR{}. Its knowledge soundness reduces to that of the FIOP and to \emph{function binding} of the commitment: no efficient prover opens one commitment to constraints that no single string satisfies. For the jagged \WHIR{} commitment, function binding rests on the collision resistance of $\Hash$ and on the soundness of \WHIR{}. We have not proved it in that form and do not quantify the loss of the reduction; it is a hypothesis of this layer, which Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} carries inside its per-node round-by-round premise, stated for the committed protocol. No figure of Table~\ref{tab:components} depends on it.

\paragraph{Layer 3: Fiat--Shamir in the random-oracle model.} In the random-oracle model a Merkle commitment admits straightline extraction, which reads the committed strings from the adversary's oracle queries without rewinding~\citep{chiesa-yogev}, so the bound carries no rewinding loss. We state the compiled level through the per-round premise of the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional composition]
\label{thm:composition}
Work in the random-oracle model, and let an adversary make at most $Q$ oracle queries. Let every proof in the tree, including each shard, global, leaf and compose proof, be the Fiat--Shamir compilation~\citep{bcs16} of an interactive protocol that is round-by-round knowledge sound with error $\varepsilon_i = \sum_c \varepsilon_{i,c}$. Let a block proof contain $n_\pi$ such proofs, each verified once by its parent, with the root checked by the external verifier, every leaf and compose key, the root's included, an entry of the allowlist, and every shard and global proof verified against the guest key $\mathsf{vk}$ (\S\ref{sec:security-binding}). Assume further that each node extractor succeeds on transcripts produced by its parent extractor, not only on transcripts drawn from an honest interaction, and that the leaf and compose programs enforce the compose relation of Section~\ref{sec:security-binding}, which no theorem of \S\ref{sec:verification} covers. Then the block proof is an argument of knowledge for $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arith}}$ with knowledge error at most
\[
  Q \Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n_\pi} \varepsilon_i \;+\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{glob}}\Big) \;+\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{hash}},
\]
where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{glob}}$ is the error of the global challenge (\S\ref{sec:global-security}) and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hash}} = O(Q^2/2^{d})$ is the probability of a collision in the $d$-bit output of $\Hash$, which builds the transcript and the Merkle trees, with $d = 248$ in the measured instantiation, the term SWIRL states explicitly for its own compilation~\citep[Cor.~4.2.6]{swirl}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Compiling a round-by-round knowledge sound protocol with Fiat--Shamir costs a factor in the number of oracle queries: an adversary may re-query the oracle on modified prefixes, and the standard analysis charges $Q$ attempts against the per-round error~\citep{bcs16,chiesa-yogev}. Within the tree, the extractor of the compiled argument runs the extractor of each verified proof in turn: a compose node's extractor yields its children's transcripts, and so on down to the shard proofs, whose extractor yields the witness rows. The allowlist hypothesis makes each step an extraction from the intended verifier; without it a node could verify a program other than the compose or leaf program. The hypothesis about parent-produced transcripts is not discharged here: a child's extractor is invoked on a transcript the parent's extractor constructed, which is not the distribution its guarantee is stated against, and a compose node checks its children inside its own circuit rather than as further rounds of one protocol. The global challenge is one further round of the block's protocol, drawn once from the chain over every shard's global round, so its error is charged once per block and, like a round of a node, re-queried at most $Q$ times. That a shard proof and its global proof open one table is a property of the commitment across two transcripts (\S\ref{sec:global-security}); it belongs with the function binding of Layer~2 and is assumed with it. Given that premise, the failure events combine by the union bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Zero knowledge}
\label{sec:zk}

The proofs above are succinct but not hiding: opened values, sumcheck messages and queried codeword entries are functions of the trace. An optional \emph{terminal proof} adds zero knowledge: one hiding proof after the recursion root, whose statement is the program's key digest, the committed-values digest, a zero exit code and the recursion root, and whose witness is the root proof. Its shard shape is fixed by the verifier it proves, so the dimensions it carries do not depend on the execution. The construction and its budgets are stated for the schedules that preceded the 128-bit ones, over the degree-four extension of \KB{}; the terminal proof has not been solved for 128 bits.

\paragraph{Construction.} The terminal proof is the shard protocol of Section~\ref{sec:argument} with four additions, each a committed source of randomness drawn before the challenge it meets.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Random rows.} Every main trace commits eight uniformly random rows past its real ones, in pairs with equal lookup values and opposite multiplicities, so that their fractions cancel in the \LogUp{} argument for every challenge; the zerocheck weighs the constraints by the equality table cut at the real height, so no constraint sees them. They add a uniform summand to the eight base-field coordinates of each column's two openings, at the \LogUp{} anchor and at the zerocheck point.
  \item \emph{Hiding opening.} The opening is the zero-knowledge proximity test of Chiesa, Fenzi and Weissenberg~\citep{cfw26} over Reed--Solomon codes, run on the stacked layout (Figure~\ref{fig:zk-opening}). Every row of every oracle begins with $T$ uniform seed coefficients, one more than the queries the oracle answers; preprocessed stripes have zero seeds, so the verifying key stays deterministic. The first oracle is the combination $G = \sum_j \eqf(\bm{bp}, j)\, S_j$ of the stripes at the jagged batch point, which evaluates to the jagged claim, so no per-stripe value is sent. Each folding sumcheck is masked, the mask's sum being sent before the combining challenge is drawn; each folded oracle re-encodes the fold with fresh seeds and masks and a uniform pad that the out-of-domain answers weigh; and the final message is disclosed under a committed uniform row.
  \item \emph{Masked sumchecks.} The zerocheck, the jagged reduction and every \LogUp{} layer run on the prover's polynomial plus $\rho\, g$, where $g(\bm x) = \sum_i g_i(x_i)$ with $g_i(0) + g_i(1) = 0$ is committed before $\rho$ is drawn, as in Libra~\citep{libra}. The values a \LogUp{} layer sends carry an offset that vanishes on the cube, and the circuit's output is hidden behind two merge layers over cancelling fractions.
  \item \emph{Cell claims.} The masks of item~3 occupy a \emph{mask region} of $2^{14}$ uniform cells inside the message of $G$. Each value the verifier learns of the region is a sparse linear functional of that message, batched into the opening's first claim with a fresh power of one challenge.
\end{enumerate}
The verifier rejects degenerate challenges: two opening points that give the random rows a weight matrix of rank below eight, a zero mask challenge, a \LogUp{} layer whose first challenge lies in $\{0,1\}$, and a jagged point that gives the masked stripe a zero coefficient. An honest prover meets such a rejection with probability below $2^{-20}$ and then proves again with fresh randomness.

\paragraph{Budgets.} Each source of randomness hides as many values as it has degrees of freedom, so the proof is hiding only while what the verifier learns stays within what was committed (Table~\ref{tab:zk-budget}). \begin{theorem}[Zero knowledge of the terminal proof]
\label{thm:zk}
Run the terminal proof with a schedule and a shard shape for which the budgets of Table~\ref{tab:zk-budget} hold, on a shard whose main round covers at least two stripes. Then the interactive protocol is honest-verifier zero knowledge: a simulator given the statement alone outputs a transcript distributed as an honest prover's. In the random-oracle model, its Fiat--Shamir compilation is zero knowledge with simulation error at most $Q\, N / 2^{371}$ against an adversary making $Q$ oracle queries, where $N = $ 1\,196\,032 is the number of leaves over the three oracles. The masks add at most 1004$/|\EF| < 2^{-113}$ to the shard's soundness error.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The simulator draws the verifier's challenges and fills the transcript backwards. Each column's two openings are uniform by the random rows; the messages of a masked sumcheck are uniform in the affine space their round identities leave; and the values a \LogUp{} layer sends are uniform given the next claim by their offsets. The opening is the construction of~\citep{cfw26} applied to a combination of stripes that each carry their own seeds, so it is simulatable given the claim: the queried rows by the seeds, the mask sums and folding messages by the stage masks, the out-of-domain answers by the pads, and the final message by its mask row; the cell claims are answered from the claimed values. The compiled proof also reveals the sibling digests of the authentication paths; every unopened leaf keeps more than 371 bits of entropy, so the simulator programs the oracle and fails only when one of the $Q$ queries hits the preimage of one of the $N$ leaves. For soundness, a misreported mask sum shifts a claim by a constant, which the challenge drawn after the commitment exposes except with probability $1/|\EF|$; with the higher degree of the masked layer rounds, the merge rounds and the batching of the cell claims, the added terms total 1004 units of $1/|\EF|$.
\end{proof}

The theorem does not cover how the terminal proof's verifying key reaches the verifier, which pins it as a constant. Measured on one CPU for the root proof of a Fibonacci guest, the hiding proof takes 2.25\,s to prove, is 571\,325 bytes and verifies in 0.15\,s; the non-hiding shrink proof of the same root takes 10 to 14\,s to prove and is 845\,701 bytes.

\subsection{Verifying-key binding}
\label{sec:security-binding}

\paragraph{The compose relation.} Write a node's public values as
\[
  \mathsf{pv} = (\mathsf{vk}, \mathsf{sh}_{\mathrm{in}}, \mathsf{sh}_{\mathrm{out}}, \mathsf{entry}, \mathsf{exit}, \mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{in}}, \mathsf{cur}_{\mathrm{out}}, g_{\mathrm{in}}, g_{\mathrm{out}}, g^\star, w, S, h, \mathsf{code}, \mathsf{root}),
\]
the guest's verifying key, the index of the node's first shard and the index after its last, its entry and exit machine states, the memory-address cursors it opens and closes with, the global chain where the node's range starts and where it ends, the chain end its global proofs were proved under and the proof-of-work witness of their global challenge, the sum of their global fractions and a count $\nu$ of the interactions they sum, the digest of committed output, the exit code, and the root of the allowlist of admissible recursion keys; a completion flag $\mathsf{complete}$, set only by the root, accompanies the tuple. The entry and exit states are \emph{pairs} $(\mathsf{pc}, \mathsf{next\_pc})$, so adjacency pins both components. A compose node of arity $k$ proves $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{comp}}(\mathsf{pv}; \pi_0,\dots,\pi_{k-1})$, which holds when every check of Algorithm~\ref{alg:compose} passes; the root's additional checks close the execution, the global chain and the cross-shard interactions, the exit program counter being the zero the halt row sets (\S\ref{sec:ext-syscall}). A \emph{shard leaf} proves the same relation for its one child, a core shard proof verified against the guest's $\mathsf{vk}$. It sets $\mathsf{sh}_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\mathsf{sh}_{\mathrm{out}}$ from the shard indices it verifies and $g_{\mathrm{out}}$ to one chain step from $g_{\mathrm{in}}$ over the global round the shard proof commits, or to $g_{\mathrm{in}}$ if it commits none. Its $S$, $\nu$, $g^\star$ and $w$ are zero: the leaf is proved before the chain ends. A \emph{global leaf} verifies one global proof, which opens consecutive global rounds within the bound of \S\ref{sec:arch-recursion}, against $\mathsf{vk}$, under the global challenge of the $(g^\star, w)$ it publishes, whose proof of work it checks and whose $w$ it requires to be non-zero. It steps the chain over the commitment and shape of each round the proof opens and publishes the chain after those rounds as $g_{\mathrm{in}}$ and the chain before them as $g_{\mathrm{out}}$, so global leaves run the chain backwards; $S$ is the proof's sum, $\nu$ counts the interactions of the rounds it opens (\S\ref{sec:global-security}), and every other coordinate stands at the start of the execution, so a global leaf composes only before the first shard. The leaf of the first shard, index~1, also requires its entry state to be $\mathsf{start}(\mathsf{vk})$, its starting cursors to be zero and $g_{\mathrm{in}}$ to be $g_0(\mathsf{vk})$.

  set $\mathsf{complete}$\;
}
\Return $\mathsf{pv}$\;
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Binding to the machine.} The statement of Section~\ref{sec:statement} concerns the advertised machine only if every leaf verified a \emph{core} proof of that machine, every compose node ran the compose program, and the external verifier checked the root under a key of that machine. Three checks give this. A compose node requires its children's recursion keys to belong to a Merkle allowlist (Algorithm~\ref{alg:compose}, line~2), whose root, a prover-supplied witness inside the tree, is exported as a public value of the root proof. A leaf verifies its shard proof against the guest key $\mathsf{vk}$ its public values carry, and every node carries that $\mathsf{vk}$ upward unchanged. The external verifier checks the root proof under the recursion key the proof names and requires that key to be in the published allowlist, the exported root to equal the published root, the completion flag $\mathsf{complete}$ to be set, and the exported $\mathsf{vk}$ to be the key of the guest program it expects (\S\ref{sec:argument-block}). Every node's key, the root's included, is thus an allowlist entry or the expected guest key. The allowlist covers leaf as well as compose programs: the shard-to-leaf step is uniform per shard shape but not across shapes, and committing to the leaf keys in advance lets one compose program accept the non-uniform set of leaves. A compose program without the membership check has a different key, which the allowlist does not contain; that the recursion programs enforce the relation is hypothesis~3 of \S\ref{sec:security-relation}.

\subsection{Security of the global challenge}
\label{sec:global-security}

\paragraph{What the chain binds.} The chain start $g_0(\mathsf{vk})$ is sampled from a transcript that has absorbed the verifying key, so the chain is tied to the guest. A step absorbs the shard's global-round commitment and the identity and height of every chip in it, so the chain end $g^\star$ fixes, up to a collision of $\Hash$, every row of every shard that carries a global interaction, and the shape of those tables, before $(\alpha_g, \beta_g)$ is drawn, after the proof-of-work witness $w$ of $b_g = 22$ bits. A global proof's transcript absorbs $g^\star$ before anything else, so it cannot be reused under another chain. The witness must be common to the global proofs, which the compose nodes enforce: with a witness per proof, each would face a challenge of its own, and a prover could search the witnesses for sums that cancel, a generalised-birthday problem over $\EF$ that is far cheaper than one search for the whole block.

\paragraph{Two proofs of one table.} The shard proof of shard $i$ shows that the tables under its global-round commitment $C_i$ satisfy the constraints and the shard's buses; a global proof shows that the tables under a commitment $C'$ of shape $\mathsf{shape}'$ have global fractions that sum to its $S$. The tree makes the two families the same. A compose node accepts a child with a non-zero $w$ only after children with the same $(g^\star, w)$, so the global leaves are the first leaves of the tree and share one challenge. In tree order they step the chain backwards over the pairs $(C', \mathsf{shape}')$ they open, from the root's $g_{\mathrm{in}}$ down to the $g_0(\mathsf{vk})$ the first shard's leaf requires, and the shard leaves step it forward from there to the root's $g_{\mathrm{out}}$ over the pairs $(C_i, \mathsf{shape}_i)$ the shard proofs commit. The root requires $g_{\mathrm{in}} = g_{\mathrm{out}} = g^\star$. Two chains from $g_0(\mathsf{vk})$ to one end absorb the same sequence of pairs unless $\Hash$ collides or cycles, so every global round has exactly one global proof, and no global proof opens anything else. A tree without a global leaf has $w = 0$ and closes only if no shard commits a global round.

Equal commitments give equal tables for two reasons. The core schedule counts the queries on its first oracle, which holds the committed stripes, in the unique-decoding regime (Table~\ref{tab:whir-schedule}), so at most one codeword lies within the tested distance of a commitment, and the two openings, made in separate transcripts, are of one polynomial; were that oracle counted in a list-decoding regime, a prover could have each proof open a different codeword near $C_i$, and both verifiers reject such a schedule. And each verifier requires the columns and padding of a round to fill exactly the area the round commits, so a column lies at the same cells of $C_i$ whether the round follows the preprocessed round, as in the shard proof, or stands alone, as in the global proof. With both, the global sums of Lemma~\ref{lem:global} are sums over the rows the shard proofs constrain.

\paragraph{The fingerprint.} A global message is a kind and at most $2^d - 1$ field values, with $d = 4$ for the core machine (a memory tuple has seven values and a system-call message eight). Its fingerprint is $f(\bm m) = \sum_{k < 2^d} \eqf(\bm\rho, k)\, v_k$, where $\bm v = (\mathsf{kind}, m_0, m_1, \dots)$ is padded with zeros and $\bm\rho \in \EF^d$ is the seed $\beta_g$ is expanded from; $f$ has total degree $d$ in $\bm\rho$, and distinct messages give distinct polynomials, since the $\eqf(\cdot, k)$ are linearly independent. Every global multiplicity is a bit, a real-row flag.

\begin{lemma}[Global sums]
\label{lem:global}
Fix the global-round tables of all shards, with $N_g$ global interactions in all, fewer than $p$ of them sends and fewer than $p$ receives, and draw $(\alpha_g, \bm\rho)$ uniformly from $\EF^{1+d}$. If the multisets of sent and received global messages differ, then the probability that every denominator $\alpha_g - f(\bm m)$ is non-zero and the shards' global sums add to zero is at most
\[
  \frac{(d+1)\, N_g}{|\EF|}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Group the fractions by message: the sums add to $\sum_{\bm u} c_{\bm u}/(\alpha_g - f(\bm u))$, where $c_{\bm u}$ is the number of sends of $\bm u$ minus its receives. Each $|c_{\bm u}| < p$, since fewer than $p$ interactions go either way, so some $c_{\bm u}$ is non-zero in $\F$. Over the field of rational functions in $\bm\rho$ the $f(\bm u)$ are distinct, so by uniqueness of partial fractions the sum is a non-zero rational function of $(\alpha_g, \bm\rho)$; its numerator over the common denominator $\prod_{\bm u}(\alpha_g - f(\bm u))$ has total degree at most $(U-1)\,d$ for $U \le N_g$ distinct messages, and vanishes with probability at most $(U-1)\,d/|\EF|$ by the Schwartz--Zippel lemma. For each $\bm\rho$, $\alpha_g$ hits one of at most $N_g$ denominator roots, those of zero-multiplicity rows included, with probability at most $N_g/|\EF|$. The union of the two events gives the bound.
\end{proof}

The lemma is the information-theoretic core. Counting the grinding inside the term it protects, as \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} does for the \LogUp{} challenge, the error of the global challenge is
\[
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{glob}} = 2^{-b_g}\, \frac{(d+1)\, N_g}{|\EF|}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} charges it $Q$ times, since a prover can re-commit global rounds and redraw $g^\star$, and adds the collision term for the chain. The global proofs and global leaves are further proofs of the tree and count among its $n_\pi$. The bound depends on the global interactions of the whole block, not of one shard. The block measured in \S\ref{sec:perf-global} has $N_g \approx 2.75 \cdot 10^8 \approx 2^{28}$, counting every padded row of a global-round chip; with $d = 4$ and $|\EF| = p^5 \approx 2^{154.9}$ the lemma's bound is about $2^{-124.6}$, and $b_g = 22$ bits of grinding give $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{glob}} \approx 2^{-146.6}$, a level above the per-proof ones of Table~\ref{tab:components}. The verifiers enforce the lemma's condition, without which $p$ copies of one message would cancel. A global round with tables of heights $h_c$, whose chips have $s_c$ sends and $r_c$ receives a row, holds at most $m = \sum_c h_c \max(s_c, r_c)$ interactions either way, every multiplicity being a bit. A global leaf computes $m$ for each round it opens from the heights the chain binds, counts the round as $\lfloor m / 2^{10} \rfloor + 1$ units and publishes the units of its rounds as $\nu$; compose nodes add the counts, and every node rejects $\nu > \nu_{\max} = (p-1)/2^{10}$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:compose}). A count that small adds without wrapping, and the rounds under an accepted root hold fewer than $2^{10}\, \nu_{\max} < p$ sends and as few receives. The bound is also a limit on the execution, about $2^{31}$ cross-shard interactions either way. Every accepted block then has $N_g < 2p$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{glob}} < 2^{-142}$. The lemma also counts on a verifier that rejects a zero denominator. The fractions a proof outputs have the products of the rows' denominators as their denominators, and both verifiers reject an output with a zero denominator; the recursion programs assert it before they divide, because their arithmetic accepts any quotient of zero by zero, which would leave the sum of a round with a zero-multiplicity row on a root of the denominator free.

\subsection{Trace uniqueness}
\label{sec:air-unique}

\begin{lemma}[Cross-shard memory]
\label{lem:xmem}
Let $(\mathsf{T}_i, \mathsf{pv}_i)_{i<N}$ satisfy $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arith}}$ with fewer than $p$ global interactions in all, and suppose the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:global} does not occur. Assume further that each shard has at most one real boundary row per word, and that such a row supplies the previous tuple of the first actual access to that word in the shard and consumes the tuple of its last actual access. Consider a shard and a word it touches. If an earlier shard touched the word, the current shard's initial boundary tuple equals the final tuple of the most recent earlier shard that touched it. If no earlier shard touched the word, the initial tuple comes from the program image for an image word and from the memory-initialisation table for any other word. The image tuples are those of the memory-image table of $\mathsf{vk}$, which the first shard sends (\S\ref{sec:air-memory}). The per-shard chains of Lemma~\ref{lem:memory} therefore join into one chain per word, ordered by $(\mathsf{shard}, \mathsf{clk})$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By the hypothesis, $\sum_i S_i = 0$ makes the sent and received multisets of global memory tuples equal, the image tuples the first shard sends included. Fix an address $a$ with $I_a$ memory-initialisation rows, $P_a$ entries in the fixed program image and $F_a$ memory-finalisation rows. Every boundary row contributes one tuple to each side at the same address, so counting multiplicities at $a$ gives $I_a + P_a = F_a \le 1$, the inequality by the strictly increasing finalisation addresses and the public cursors that chain their tables across shards. There is thus at most one initial tuple per address, with timestamp $(0,0)$, and image entries are disjoint from initialisation rows. Under the boundary-row premise, Lemma~\ref{lem:memory} makes each shard's accesses to $a$ one path from its boundary row's initial tuple to its final tuple, strictly increasing in $(\mathsf{shard},\mathsf{clk})$. The boundary paths form a finite directed graph with balanced internal tuple multiplicities, at most one initial tuple and at most one finalisation tuple; increasing timestamps exclude cycles, and the unit initial multiplicity and balance then force one path, in which consecutive segments link the last access of one touching shard to the first access of the next. This is offline memory checking~\citep{blum1994} across shards.
\end{proof}

The boundary-row premise is not proved by this argument or by the determinism theorems. The honest prover emits one boundary row per touched word, but the constraints of the shard-boundary chip do not enforce uniqueness or a nonempty access segment: a row with identical initial and final tuples contributes cancelling interactions to both the memory bus and the global interactions. This is an open obligation of item~4 in \S\ref{sec:security-relation}.

\begin{theorem}[From chip determinism to trace uniqueness]
\label{thm:unique}
Fix a program image, public values and a valid shard with the well-formed boundary rows assumed by Lemma~\ref{lem:xmem}. If every chip of the shard is deterministic in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:det}, then the rows reachable from the entry state along the state and memory buses are determined by the program image, the public values, the initial values of the words the shard touches (the hint words of the memory-initialisation table and, in a shard after the first, the initial tuples of its boundary rows), and the declared free values of Definition~\ref{def:det} (Table~\ref{tab:det-findings}): any two valid shards that agree on these agree on those rows up to permutation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:chain} the instruction rows of either shard form one chain $r_1, \dots, r_N$ from the entry state; let a step $i$ be $r_i$ together with the precompile rows it invokes, which access memory at $r_i$'s clock. We show by induction on $i$ that the rows of step $i$ have the same inputs and outputs in both shards. The inputs of $r_i$ are its received state tuple $s_{i-1}$, which is public for $i = 1$ and otherwise the output of $r_{i-1}$; its fetched instruction, a function of $\mathsf{pc}(s_{i-1})$ because the program bus is received only by the committed program table; and the previous values of the addresses it accesses. By Lemma~\ref{lem:memory} such a value is the one written by the last earlier access to that address, which belongs to a step $j < i$, or the word's initial value, which is fixed: an image word through the memory-image table of $\mathsf{vk}$, a hint word or boundary tuple by the statement. So the inputs of $r_i$ agree, and determinism of its chip gives that its outputs agree; the same argument applies to the step's precompile rows, which receive their arguments from $r_i$'s syscall tuple, in the order of their accesses.

Rows of non-instruction chips (the memory-initialisation and finalisation tables, the shadow reads, the tables) are determined in the same way from the bus tuples they receive, which are outputs of instruction rows or public. Rows on no bus reachable from the entry state are not covered; the arithmetisation admits none, because every real row carries a frame or a memory access, but that is a property of the chip set rather than of the buses, and we do not prove it here.
\end{proof}

None of the declared free values makes the execution ambiguous: the address and value of a memory-initialisation row are the initial value of a touched word, already in the list, and the length of a hint read is part of the untrusted hint. Applied to the shards in index order, the theorem extends to the whole execution, since by Lemma~\ref{lem:xmem} the initial tuples of a shard's boundary rows are final tuples of earlier shards. The theorem is argued on paper; chip determinism, its main premise, is machine-checked for the chips of \S\ref{sec:verification-det}.
