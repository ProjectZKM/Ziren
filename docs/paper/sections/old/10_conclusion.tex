\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\Ziren{} is the first \zkVM{} for the MIPS instruction set, a mature architecture deployed in billions of devices and shared with the fault-proof infrastructure of Ethereum rollups, and this paper has described its second generation as a processor: instruction units carrying their own frames on a state bus, one multiset argument for registers and memory with an elliptic-curve digest across shards, one lookup argument and one zerocheck per shard, a commitment that stacks a shard into one polynomial opened through the jagged reduction and \WHIR{}, and a verification flow of specification-derived vectors, an executable \Lean{} model and extracted determinism theorems. On one RTX~5090 an Ethereum block of 288 million MIPS cycles proves to a 603\,KiB compressed proof in 74\,s with the deployed configuration, 67\,s with the changes evaluated here and 49\,s after the lookup-layer change; four GPUs in one host reach 20\,MHz of proved execution.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The proofs are not zero-knowledge. The soundness statement rests on a computational assumption for the cross-shard digest in addition to the information-theoretic bounds of the proximity test, and the numerical level is reported through the artifact's accounting rather than restated here. The determinism theorems cover the core machine and not the recursion machine, and a substantial fraction of them remain open under the automation's budget (Figure~\ref{fig:verification}); the lemma connecting unit determinism to trace uniqueness is stated, not machine-checked. The evaluation uses a single workload class (Ethereum blocks) on a single GPU model. The verifying-key allowlist that binds the recursion to the advertised machine is collected from executions for the core programs and therefore covers the block guest rather than an arbitrary program.

\paragraph{Future work.} The measurements identify the next levers in the arithmetisation rather than in the proximity test: the number of \emph{(row, interaction)} pairs the lookup circuit processes, the two \texttt{Global} rows that every shard-touched word costs, and the memory footprint of the lookup circuit's first layer, which bounds the shard size. On the verification side, the open determinism theorems call for reasoning modulo $p$ in the tactic and for a compositional treatment of the accelerator units; the bus lemma should be machine-checked; and the recursion machine should be brought under the same extraction. On the application side, the case for MIPS made in Section~\ref{sec:why-mips} should be tested on firmware-class workloads from the embedded fleet rather than on the block-proving benchmark alone.
