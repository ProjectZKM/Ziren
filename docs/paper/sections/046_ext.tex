\subsection{Chips for the extended instruction set and system calls}
\label{sec:ext}

Each of these instructions is still one row of one chip, which carries as columns the work a lookup-based design spreads over a sequence of virtual instructions~\citep{jolt}. \texttt{HI} is written by a second access at sub-cycle position 4, which raises eight interactions: a send and a receive on the memory bus, two timestamp lookups, and two byte lookups each for its previous and its new word. We work multiplication in full and summarise the rest (Table~\ref{tab:chips}).

\paragraph{Multiplication and division.}\label{sec:ext-mul}\label{sec:ext-div} A 64-bit product exceeds the field, so it is formed as a convolution of bytes. Both operands are extended to eight bytes, with $\mathtt{0xFF}$ times the sign bit for the signed forms and zeros otherwise; the sign extension does in the row what Jolt's virtual sequence for signed high multiplication does~\citep{jolt}. The uncarried convolution $m_k = \sum_{i+j=k} b_i c_j$, $k < 8$, is tied to witnessed product bytes $\pi_k$ and carries $\theta_k$ by $m_k + \theta_{k-1} = \pi_k + 256\,\theta_k$, with carries range-checked to 16 bits and product bytes to 8. The low word $\pi_{0..3}$ goes to $\mathsf{op\_a}$ and the high word to \texttt{HI}. The multiply--accumulate instructions add the same product to the 64-bit $(\texttt{HI}, \texttt{LO})$ with a byte-carry addition, and prove a subtraction as the addition that undoes it.

For a division the prover supplies the quotient $q$ and remainder $r$ as advice, as in Jolt~\citep{jolt}, and the row checks them: $qc + r = b$ by the same convolution and a byte-carry addition, the high word of the sum being the sign extension of $b$ except in the overflowing case $b = -2^{31}$, $c = -1$; that $r$ takes the dividend's sign; $|r| < |c|$ on absolute values; and $c \neq 0$, division by zero being an execution error (\S\ref{sec:isa-emulator}). Jolt's check is eight virtual instructions; here it is one row with 131 columns beyond the frame.

\paragraph{Bit manipulation and conditional moves.}\label{sec:ext-bits} The leading-zero count $a$ of a word $b$ is 32 for the zero word and is otherwise proved by showing, with the right-shift construction of \S\ref{sec:base-shift} inlined, that shifting $b$ right by $31 - a$ leaves exactly 1; a leading-one count is that of the complemented word. The conditional moves test $c = 0$ by witnessed inverses and select between $b$ and the previous value of $\mathsf{op\_a}$, which the frame's read--write access carries; their chip also proves the byte swap \texttt{wsbh}, a permutation of bytes. A bit-field extract is a left shift followed by a right shift, and an insert rotates the destination, shifts, adds the shifted source and rotates back. The rare opcodes (the bit-field instructions, the sign extensions, the multiply--accumulate instructions and \texttt{teq}) share one \emph{rare-instruction chip}, in which the witnesses of different opcodes occupy the same columns, so the row is as wide as the insert needs, 432 columns, and every lookup is gated by an opcode selector. \texttt{teq} is provable only when it does not trap.

\paragraph{System calls.}\label{sec:ext-syscall} \texttt{syscall} is the one place a row hands work to another table. The call number is read as bytes: the low two are the call identifier, the third says whether the call is sent to a table, and the fourth is a count of extra clock ticks, by which the frame advances the clock beyond five so that a precompile's memory accesses fit before the successor. The two arguments travel on the syscall bus as exact 16-bit halves. A halting call sets the next program counter to zero and exposes its first argument as the public exit code; a commit call compares its argument with the public output digest. A Linux-call chip receives the Linux-ABI calls of Appendix~\ref{sec:app-isa} and performs their memory accesses at the calling cycle, and the precompile chips of Appendix~\ref{sec:app-chips} receive accelerator calls, routed across shards as global interactions or, with Merkle roots, proved in the shard that issues them (\S\ref{sec:air-global}).
