\section{The \WHIR{} opening}
\label{sec:iopp}

\WHIR{}~\citep{whir} opens the jagged commitment of Section~\ref{sec:pcs} (the commitment $\mathsf{JC}$ of \S\ref{sec:argument-components}). It authenticates the claim $v_Q = \widetilde Q(\bm z^{*})$ left by the jagged reduction, as an interactive oracle proof of proximity (IOPP) for \emph{constrained} Reed--Solomon codes, whose multilinear also satisfies a stated weighted sum. This section gives the protocol as implemented and the commitment layout; the soundness of the schedule is accounted in \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness}. The schedule is the opening's principal parameter: at a fixed soundness level it trades proof size against prover time.

\subsection{The protocol}
\label{sec:iopp-protocol}

\WHIR{} tests membership of $f : L \to \F$ in $\CRS[\F, L, m, \widehat w, \sigma]$ (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}). An iteration with folding factor $k$ runs $k$ sumcheck rounds, sends a folded oracle re-encoded at the round's rate, answers out-of-domain samples, answers $t$ shift queries by opening $2^k$ symbols each, and recurses on a constrained code with $k$ fewer variables; the final polynomial is sent in the clear. The out-of-domain samples distinguish \WHIR{} from BaseFold: they pin the folded oracle to a unique nearby codeword before the shift queries are drawn. At the same provable level \WHIR{} needs fewer queries in later rounds than BaseFold and a handful of committed rounds rather than one per variable, while sharing its prover skeleton (encode, Merkle-commit, fold).

\subsection{Commitment layout}
\label{sec:iopp-commit}

\Zirenver{} uses the \emph{interleaved} commitment: a polynomial in $\nu$ variables is reshaped into a $2^{\nu-k_0} \times 2^{k_0}$ matrix whose column $c$ is the stride-$2^{k_0}$ slice $f[h \cdot 2^{k_0} + c]$; each column is Reed--Solomon encoded independently (one DFT per column), and, for a single stripe, Merkle leaf $j$ holds the $2^{k_0}$ column values at domain point $x_j$. Folding a leaf by the round's challenge gives the folded polynomial at $x_j$, so a query answer enters the next sumcheck as an ordinary constraint whose weight folds in closed form.

The layout is applied separately to every input-commitment round of Section~\ref{sec:pcs-stacking}; the roots are not merged. Within a root the stripes are grouped 32 to a leaf, so a leaf of the first oracle authenticates one coset row from each stripe, $32 \times 2^{k_0}$ field elements. The first folding factor $k_0$ is therefore small: leaf width multiplies every first-round query, and re-hashing those leaves is the recursion verifier's dominant cost. Later rounds query a single folded polynomial and fold more variables at a time. Each committed round re-encodes the folded polynomial at its new rate; the opening of a \texttt{reth} shard costs about 39\,ms of host-visible time on the GPU with device-resident rounds, the round-0 re-encoding being the largest term. No proof-of-work is spent on folding challenges; grinding is spent only on the query phases and on the batching and \LogUp{} challenges.

Table~\ref{tab:whir-schedule} gives one schedule, the one the soundness accounting of \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} is computed for; \S\ref{sec:perf-proofsize} describes how the schedule controls proof size. The first oracle has rate $2^{-2}$, and each recommitment lowers the rate by three powers of two. At log stacking height 21 the three folds consume 15 variables, and the remaining six-variable polynomial is sent in the clear after its final query phase.
