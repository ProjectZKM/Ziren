\section{The \WHIR{} opening}
\label{sec:iopp}

\WHIR{}~\citep{whir} opens the jagged commitment of Section~\ref{sec:pcs} (the commitment $\mathsf{JC}$ of \S\ref{sec:argument-components}). It authenticates the claim $v_Q = \widetilde Q(\bm z^{*})$ left by the jagged reduction, as an interactive oracle proof of proximity (IOPP) for \emph{constrained} Reed--Solomon codes, whose multilinear also satisfies a stated weighted sum. Its schedule trades proof size against prover time at a fixed soundness level, which \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} accounts.

\subsection{The protocol}
\label{sec:iopp-protocol}

\WHIR{} tests membership of $f : L \to \F$ in $\CRS[\F, L, m, \widehat w, \sigma]$ (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}). An iteration with folding factor $k$ runs $k$ sumcheck rounds, sends a folded oracle re-encoded at the round's rate, answers out-of-domain samples, answers $t$ shift queries by opening $2^k$ symbols each, and recurses on a constrained code with $k$ fewer variables; the final polynomial is sent in the clear. The out-of-domain samples pin the folded oracle to a unique nearby codeword before the shift queries are drawn.

\subsection{Commitment layout and schedule}
\label{sec:iopp-commit}

\Ziren{} uses the \emph{interleaved} commitment: a polynomial in $\nu$ variables is reshaped into a $2^{\nu-k_0} \times 2^{k_0}$ matrix whose column $c$ is the stride-$2^{k_0}$ slice $f[h \cdot 2^{k_0} + c]$; each column is Reed--Solomon encoded independently, and, for a single stripe, Merkle leaf $j$ holds the $2^{k_0}$ column values at domain point $x_j$. Folding a leaf by the round's challenge gives the folded polynomial at $x_j$, so a query answer enters the next sumcheck as an ordinary constraint whose weight folds in closed form.

The layout is applied separately to every input-commitment round of Section~\ref{sec:pcs-stacking}, and one batched \WHIR{} opening authenticates all input rounds against their respective roots. Within each input round's Merkle tree, a leaf of the first oracle holds one coset row from every stripe under that root, $S_r \times 2^{k_0}$ field elements. The first folding factor $k_0$ is therefore small: leaf width multiplies every first-round query, and re-hashing those leaves is the recursion verifier's dominant cost. Later rounds query a single folded polynomial and fold more variables at a time.

Table~\ref{tab:whir-schedule} gives the two schedules of the compressed proof, the core schedule, which the core shards and every recursion proof below the root use, and the compress schedule of the root; \S\ref{sec:perf-proofsize} describes how a schedule controls proof size. Every schedule is solved so that every component of the opening has at least 128 bits (\S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness}), and each recommitment lowers the rate by three powers of two. The core schedule is tuned for prover time: it starts at rate $2^{-1}$, and at log stacking height 21 its three folds consume 15 variables, the remaining six-variable polynomial being sent in the clear after its final query phase. It counts the queries on its first oracle, the stripe trees, in the unique-decoding regime, on which the global challenge relies (\S\ref{sec:global-security}) and under which the folds of the first round need no grinding; the oracles it commits later are counted under the Johnson bound with multiplicity $m = 3$, and each of their folding challenges grinds what its own error needs, 4 to 11 bits. The compress schedule, under which the root of the recursion tree proves, is tuned for the size of the published proof: it starts at the lowest rate the two-adicity of \KB{} admits at this stacking height, counts every oracle under the Johnson bound, folds two variables first so that a first-round query opens half the leaf, and grinds every folding and batching challenge, which the list size of the Johnson-bound analysis requires.

\paragraph{Proximity regimes.} Each oracle is counted in one of two regimes, and neither uses a proximity-gap conjecture. In the unique-decoding regime the list size is one, and the calculator of \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} evaluates in its source the correlated-agreement error $(\delta n + 1)/|\EF|$ of~\citep[Cor.~1.4]{proximity-gaps-2025} for a code of length $n$. Under the Johnson bound with multiplicity $m$ it sets $\delta = 1 - (1 + \tfrac{1}{2m})\sqrt\rho$ and evaluates, citing Theorem~4.2 of the same paper, the error
\[
  \frac{1}{|\EF|}\Big(\frac{2(m+\tfrac12)^5 + 3(m+\tfrac12)\,\delta\rho}{3\rho^{3/2}}\; n + \frac{m+\tfrac12}{\sqrt\rho}\Big),
\]
linear in $n$, times a factor that counts the batched functions. \WHIR{} needs the mutual form of correlated agreement, which its authors conjectured up to the Johnson bound and which is now a theorem with the same expression~\citep[Thm.~4.6 and \S4.3]{proximity-gaps-2025}. The results of 2025 and 2026 that refute proximity-gap conjectures concern distances up to capacity~\citep{cs25}, and the negative results of~\citep{proximity-gaps-2025} are for characteristic two, for radii beyond list decoding, or for vanishing relative distance; none concerns these two regimes. Fenzi and Sanso list an earlier public configuration of \Ziren{} among deployed systems that used capacity-style conjectures; for its parameters, the degree-four extension at rate $1/2$ with 84 queries, their bound gives 76.4 bits against 84 conjectured, by an attack they describe as not computationally efficient~\citep[Tables~1 and~2]{fs26}. The schedules of Table~\ref{tab:whir-schedule} replace that configuration.

\paragraph{Terminal schedules.} The two stages after the root have schedules of their own, solved in the same way. The shrink stage starts at rate $2^{-2}$, counts its first oracle under unique decoding and the later ones under the Johnson bound with $m = 3$, folds $[3,6,6]$ variables with $157$ and $47$ queries and $29$ in the final phase, grinds its folding challenges by 0 bits in the first round and 7 to 13 bits later, and grinds its queries by 22 bits. The wrap stage starts at rate $2^{-3}$, counts every oracle under the Johnson bound with $m = 3$, folds $[2,4,4,4]$ variables with $82$, $38$ and $25$ queries and $19$ in the final phase, grinds its folding challenges by 9 to 22 bits and its queries by 24 bits.
