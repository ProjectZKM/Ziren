\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:performance}

We evaluate \emph{instruction efficiency}, the committed area and bus interactions an executed instruction costs (\S\ref{sec:perf-estimate}); \emph{proving efficiency}, the rate at which that area becomes a proof on named hardware (\S\ref{sec:perf-prover}); and \emph{proof size} (\S\ref{sec:perf-proofsize}). One workload family, one GPU model and one host are used, with interleaved repetitions. No cross-system comparison is attempted: other systems' figures are self-reported on different guests and hardware.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:perf-setup}

\paragraph{Workload.} Ethereum mainnet blocks executed by MIPS32 builds of two execution clients, Reth and the block keeper of Geth; each guest hashes trie nodes, recomputes a Merkle root over state it does not hold and verifies signatures. The census of \S\ref{sec:perf-profile} uses block 25\,955\,640 (495.6\,M cycles), Table~\ref{tab:clients} the 15 consecutive blocks 26\,138\,416 to 26\,138\,430, and \S\ref{sec:perf-skip} and Table~\ref{tab:cards} 25 blocks in the range 26\,413\,605 to 26\,413\,714 with the Reth guest, of 72 to 579\,M cycles and 8.31\,G in total.

\paragraph{Hardware.} NVIDIA RTX~5090 GPUs (32\,GB) in a virtual machine with eight of them, 124 vCPUs of AMD EPYC 9355 and 925\,GB of memory. Table~\ref{tab:cards} uses one, two and four GPUs and Table~\ref{tab:clients} one and all eight (driver 570.153.02, CUDA 12.8). The GPU backend is a separate CUDA implementation of the same protocol.

\paragraph{Configurations.}\label{sec:perf-revision} Sections~\ref{sec:air}--\ref{sec:security} describe the \emph{analysed configuration}, and Table~\ref{tab:clients} measures it, with one difference that holds for every measurement of this section outside \S\ref{sec:perf-global}: cross-shard interactions are carried by the elliptic-curve multiset hash that the global challenge replaced (\S\ref{sec:air-global}), so its cross-shard digest chip appears in the area figures and the global proofs in none of the timings. The kernel profile of Table~\ref{tab:census} and the ablations of \S\ref{sec:perf-prover} were taken on an earlier \emph{measured configuration}, which used 13 partial rounds of $\Poseidon{}$ rather than the corrected 20, did not grind the lookup challenge, and lacked the three soundness fixes of Table~\ref{tab:det-findings} and the enumeration of accepted system calls (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-isa}); comparisons between its runs are internal to it. The measurements of \S\ref{sec:perf-skip}, Table~\ref{tab:cards} and \S\ref{sec:perf-proofsize} are of the \emph{current configuration}: the analysed one with the area reductions of \S\ref{sec:perf-changes} and the univariate refinement of \S\ref{sec:air-skip}, whose sumcheck term is that of Equation~\eqref{eq:skip-round-error}. Areas are of the analysed configuration unless stated. The schedules of Table~\ref{tab:whir-schedule} and the levels of \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} are those of the \emph{128-bit configuration}; every measurement of this section that does not name it was taken with the schedules that preceded it, solved for 106 bits per component over the degree-four extension of \KB{}, and \S\ref{sec:perf-grind} gives the difference.

\paragraph{Metrics and protocol.} \emph{Trace area} (\S\ref{sec:prelim-chips}) is counted when a shard closes. \emph{Proving time} runs from the prove request to the compressed proof, over shard proving and recursion, and excludes the client's execution and verification. \emph{Proving throughput} is guest cycles divided by proving time, in MHz. All proofs are warm, and single-block rows report the mean of three consecutive proofs. Every proof contributing to Tables~\ref{tab:cards} and~\ref{tab:clients} was checked by a native verifier. Table~\ref{tab:clients} is of 11 October 2026 and the current-configuration runs of 24 November 2026, the latter with unrelated light workloads on other GPUs of the host.

\paragraph{Per instruction.} The frame is most of every common row: an addition or bitwise operation pays 29--32 cells and 16--20 interactions of frame for at most five cells and four interactions of its own, so the per-instruction floor is set by the register memory check. Expensive instructions are expensive in columns, not rows: a division is one row of 163 cells where Jolt's sequence takes eight steps~\citep{jolt}. Per-opcode chips keep the common rows narrow, as in SP1~\citep{sp1}: one table carrying every opcode's columns would pay over 400 cells on every instruction.

\paragraph{Per block.}\label{sec:perf-profile} Block 25\,955\,640 commits 29.2\,G cells and raises 12.8\,G (row, interaction) pairs, 58.9 cells and 25.8 pairs per executed instruction (Table~\ref{tab:census}, Figure~\ref{fig:area}). This exceeds the single-row costs mainly because of the per-shard memory-argument rows: the cross-shard digest chip holds 15\% of the area, because every word a shard touches costs two rows of it, rows the global challenge removes. The figure depends on the workload: a precompile call is one guest cycle and many rows of an accelerator chip, and a shard closes at an area budget (\S\ref{sec:isa-record}).

\paragraph{Effect of reducing area.}\label{sec:perf-changes} The census names two quantities to reduce: trace cells, for the commitment and the constraint argument, and (row, interaction) pairs, for the bus argument. The current configuration (\S\ref{sec:perf-revision}) reduces both without changing the statement proved. The cross-shard digest chip replaces a boolean sign decomposition by three byte lookups. A store takes the word it writes from the register access of the same row, and a taken branch or jump proves its target addition on the word it sends on the state bus, constraining only the carries. A register access no longer witnesses a limb of its timestamp difference: the register shadow-read chip also inserts a read before any access that follows the register's previous one by $2^{16}$ clock ticks or more, so the difference is one 16-bit quantity, range-checked with one lookup, and the access falls from six columns and two lookups to five and one. The R-type frame thereby falls from 30 columns and 20 interactions to 27 and 16; an addition row is 33 cells and 16 interactions against the 36 and 20 of Table~\ref{tab:estimate}, a word store 46 and 20 against 52 and 25, and a branch 50 and at most 18 against 56 and at most 25. These changes alter the constraint system, and with it the chip keys and the set of admissible recursion keys.

\paragraph{Where the time goes.} In the measured configuration the lookup argument is the largest consumer of kernel time (Table~\ref{tab:census}, Figure~\ref{fig:kernel}), and its cost scales with (row, interaction) pairs rather than with columns: memory-instruction chips carry 25--26 interactions per row and 47\% of all pairs, while the cross-shard digest chip holds 15\% of the area but 3.7\% of the pairs. The cost model is therefore area for the commitment and zerocheck and pairs for the lookup, with one pair weighted as about two cells in this profile.

\paragraph{Layer order and shard size.}\label{sec:perf-after} Pairing the fractions of the first \LogUp{} layer on the least-significant index bit lets each chip fold out after $\log_2$ of its own height, where pairing on the most-significant bit carried short chips through every layer of the tallest; the memory this frees admits shards of twice the size. With both, a 288\,M-cycle block proves in 48.7\,s on one GPU against 67.8\,s without either ($-28\%$, measured configuration), with the compressed proof unchanged in size.

\paragraph{Univariate skip and narrower lookups.}\label{sec:perf-skip} The univariate refinement (\S\ref{sec:air-skip}) replaces the four largest zerocheck rounds, and the folds between them, by one round over sixteen-row blocks followed by one $16{:}1$ fold; the reductions of \S\ref{sec:perf-changes} remove (row, interaction) pairs from every instruction row. We measured the two together against the configuration without them, with the arms interleaved in the order A, B, B, A on two GPUs over the 25 blocks of \S\ref{sec:perf-setup}. Total proving time falls from 436.8\,s to 398.6\,s ($-8.7\%$), the median block from 17.2\,s to 15.7\,s and the 90th percentile from 25.0\,s to 22.9\,s; every block is faster, the repeated arms agree within 2.6\%, and zerocheck kernel time falls by 28\%. On four GPUs the same comparison gives 388.0\,s against 344.2\,s ($-11.3\%$). The refinement leaves the chips' constraints unchanged; it changes the shard verifier, and with it the recursion programs and the set of admissible recursion keys.

\paragraph{Scaling across devices.}\label{sec:perf-scaling} Table~\ref{tab:cards} and Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} report the current configuration on one, two and four GPUs. One GPU proves the 25 blocks at 14.0\,MHz, with a median of 22.6\,s per block. Two GPUs are $1.51\times$ faster: 21.2\,MHz, a median of 15.1\,s, a 90th percentile of 22.4\,s and 29.8\,s for the slowest block. Four add little: 357.4\,s against 391.5\,s, with a median of 13.7\,s. One GPU completes the set in 592.6\,s, so, the device work being the same, the two GPUs of the 391.5\,s run are idle for at least 24\% of their time and the four of the 357.4\,s run for at least 59\%. We attribute the remainder to work that does not spread over devices: each shard is re-executed and its traces generated on the host before a GPU can prove it (\S\ref{sec:perf-host}), and the top of the recursion tree is serial (\S\ref{sec:perf-pipeline}). The analysed configuration, which proved a 348\,M-cycle block in 55.1, 29.2 and 16.4\,s on one, two and four GPUs, was further from that bound: since then the single-GPU time of a block of that size has more than halved, and the four-GPU time has fallen by about 12\%.

\paragraph{Cross-shard options.}\label{sec:perf-global} Table~\ref{tab:pq} gives the cost of the options of \S\ref{sec:air-global} relative to the elliptic-curve multiset hash. Over ten blocks of 72 to 579 million cycles proved with the Reth guest, the global challenge costs 0.3\,\% on one GPU, 4.5\,\% on two and 13\,\% on four. The cost is the global proofs, 54 a block on average, which are proved after the block's last shard and verified eight to a leaf. It is close to a fixed time per block, about one second on four GPUs whatever the block's size, so it weighs more as more GPUs shorten the rest; on two GPUs the largest block is proved faster than under the elliptic-curve hash, in 21.7\,s against 23.3\,s. A prover that committed every global round in a pass of its own before proving any shard cost 37\,\% on four GPUs. The traces of the global round are generated on the GPUs. The global challenge keeps the independent precompile and memory shards of the elliptic-curve multiset hash and the machine's memory chips, and removes the rows of the cross-shard digest chip.

\paragraph{The host.}\label{sec:perf-emu}\label{sec:perf-host} On one core of the host the executor runs block 25\,955\,640 at 198\,MHz when it compiles the guest and at 14.6\,MHz when it interprets it and records the events a trace needs. On one GPU, emulation does not limit proving. It does when one host feeds several GPUs, because each shard is re-executed by the worker that proves it (\S\ref{sec:arch-execution}): in the current configuration the GPUs wait for at least a quarter of the time on two GPUs and more than half on four (\S\ref{sec:perf-scaling}).

\paragraph{Prover cost of the soundness target.}\label{sec:perf-grind} The level of \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} is paid for in queries and in proof-of-work. With the arms interleaved on one block on four GPUs, the $22/22/14$-bit query, \LogUp{} and batching grinds took $33.4$\,s against $33.9$\,s for $16/16/8$, within run-to-run spread, while buying the same bits with queries cost $8.6$\%. That holds only because the search runs on the device: with the grinds on the host the same schedule took $166$\,s. The 128-bit configuration, with the degree-five extension, the lower first rate and the grinding of its folding challenges, costs 19\,\% on two GPUs and 13\,\% on four: over 35 blocks of 38 to 579 million cycles proved with the Reth guest, each configuration proved twice in one job, the times to the compressed proof sum to 566 and 557\,s against 489 and 453\,s on two GPUs, and to 371 and 374\,s against 330 and 329\,s on four.

\subsection{Controlling proof size}
\label{sec:perf-proofsize}

The published object is the compressed proof at the root of the tree, whose size does not grow with the execution. The query openings of \WHIR{} are 57\,\% of it and those of the first oracle 33\,\%, because each of its queries authenticates one coset row of every stripe, and the \LogUp{} proof is 24\,\%; to first order the size of the opening is
\[
  t_0 \cdot \big(\text{stripes} \cdot 2^{k_0} + \text{roots} \cdot \log_2(\text{leaves}) \cdot |\text{digest}|\big) + \textstyle\sum_{i \ge 1} t_i \cdot (\text{folded leaf} + \text{path}),
\]
with $t_i$ the queries of round $i$ and $k_0$ the first folding factor. A lower rate cuts every round's queries at a prover cost that grows as $1/\rho$; each bit of grinding removes about one bit's worth of queries at a doubling of a search that is cheap on the device (\S\ref{sec:perf-grind}); and a smaller $k_0$ narrows every first-round leaf. These parameters need to apply only to the published proof: the core shards and every recursion node below the root keep the schedule tuned for prover time, and the root alone, a compose of arity one whose committed area is the same for every tree (\S\ref{sec:argument-block}), proves under the compress schedule of Table~\ref{tab:whir-schedule}. That schedule opens $45 \cdot 2^{2}$ values per stripe in the first oracle against $256 \cdot 2^{3}$ for the core schedule, under a tenth, with every component of both at 128 bits or more (Table~\ref{tab:components}), for 14 to 27 bits of grinding on each of its challenges, paid once per proof.

In the 128-bit configuration, with a Fibonacci guest, the compressed proof is 284\,330 bytes (278\,KiB), independent of the guest's execution length. Those of the 35 blocks of \S\ref{sec:perf-grind} are 284\,396 bytes and verify natively in a median of 31\,ms, against 289\,980 bytes and 20\,ms under the preceding schedules in the same job, both without the child-key allowlist checks; the proof is smaller at the higher level because the root starts at rate $2^{-5}$ rather than $2^{-3}$. In the current configuration, with a Fibonacci guest, the compressed proof is 291\,162 bytes (284\,KiB) and native verification takes 17.7\,ms (median of five warm runs on the host of \S\ref{sec:perf-setup}; 16.4--20.3\,ms over six repetitions), with the prover's in-circuit child-key checks and the verifier's root-key check enabled. Without the univariate refinement the same measurement, taken alongside, gives 290\,530 bytes and 22.0\,ms. The size grows because the 61-coefficient first message replaces four messages of five coefficients. The time falls because the verifier evaluates the jagged indicator at each column's integer offsets, where a layer of the branching program costs at most twelve multiplications; this more than offsets the sixteen-way first layer the refinement adds (\S\ref{sec:pcs-skip}), with which alone the verification took 30.4\,ms.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:perf-threats}

\emph{Internal.} The spread of the interleaved repetitions is small relative to the effects reported, but unrelated workloads shared the host during the current-configuration runs. \emph{Construct.} The kernel profile and the ablations predate the $\Poseidon{}$ correction; they are evidence about mechanisms to the extent the correction leaves relative costs unchanged. The multi-GPU figures of the current configuration are bounded by the host rather than by the devices (\S\ref{sec:perf-scaling}), and the eight-GPU columns of Table~\ref{tab:clients} have not been repeated in it. \emph{External.} The workload is Ethereum block execution, and other guests have different instruction mixes.
