\subsection{From constraints to MIPS32: conformance and determinism}
\label{sec:verification}

Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} an accepting proof implies a trace satisfying every constraint, and Theorem~\ref{thm:unique} makes that trace unique given chip determinism. That the trace is the MIPS32 execution needs two further properties. The constraints must compute the instruction set's function, since a chip that computes the wrong one is sound for the wrong machine; the evidence is conformance testing against an independent reference (\S\ref{sec:verification-conformance}). And they must admit only that function, since a chip with two outputs for one input lets a cheating prover choose, which honest traces never expose; this is proved in \Lean{}, chip by chip (\S\ref{sec:verification-det}).

\paragraph{Trusted base.} Soundness rests only on the verifier and what it reads: every chip's constraint description, the preprocessed tables and program commitment in the verifying key, the recursion programs, and the allowlist root. Execution, trace generation and the GPU prover bear only on completeness: an error there yields a proof that fails to verify, not one that verifies falsely. The evidence below covers the core chips; the recursion programs are a hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}.

\paragraph{Declared shapes.}\label{sec:verification-forgery} The committed geometry is quantised from declared heights (\S\ref{sec:pcs-geometry}), so the argument must reject a shard whose declared shape is false. As a negative control, valid shard proofs were altered in eighteen ways (over- and under-claimed heights, false chip activity, changed column and row counts, in the proof, in the transcript or in both). Every case is rejected: by the last-layer reconstruction of the \LogUp{} argument, by the grinding witness, by the jagged verifier, or by the preprocessed round, whose row counts come from the verifying key.

\subsubsection{ISA conformance}
\label{sec:verification-conformance}
\label{sec:verification-isa}

The encoding tables of the MIPS32 manual were transcribed into a machine-readable specification of the 77 instructions (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-isa}), from which ten test programs per instruction are generated, with pseudo-random operands, boundary values and the delay-slot placements the instruction admits. The reference is the Unicorn/QEMU MIPS32 emulator~\citep{unicorn}, an independent implementation; each vector compares the general registers, \texttt{HI}/\texttt{LO} and touched memory, and all 770 agree, under both the interpreter and the just-in-time compiler. Hand-written suites add 144 cases for instructions with difficult semantics, such as division, multiply-accumulate, bit-field instructions and rotates, and the test programs of Optimism's Cannon~\citep{cannon}, another team's reading of the manual, add the 49 that apply to a little-endian guest; all pass.

Of these, 224 generated programs and 48 of the 49 Cannon programs were also proved and verified end to end. The exception halts with a non-zero exit code, which the emulator accepts and the arithmetisation does not admit. An executable \Lean{} model of the instruction set, written independently of the emulator, agrees with the emulator's decoder on all 2\,275 distinct instruction words of the vectors and reproduces the reference results on all 770. On the tested executions the arithmetisation thus accepts every behaviour the reference exhibits; the determinism below gives part of the converse, that it admits at most one behaviour per input.

\subsubsection{Determinism of the chips}
\label{sec:verification-det}

\paragraph{Statement.} The statement for a chip $U$ is Definition~\ref{def:det} for its constraint system, with byte-table lookups lowered to range constraints or to an abstract table relation. A theorem that uses such a relation assumes it to be a function, which holds because the verifying key fixes the table; 32 of the \ndettotal{} theorems do, for the bitwise and shift-carry operations of the byte table. The inputs include the declared free values, which Table~\ref{tab:det-findings} records. These theorems are the determinism premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:unique}.

\paragraph{Method.} The constraint description that the prover evaluates and the verifier checks is evaluated once with symbolic columns and lowered to a first-order statement in \Lean{}, one per chip or per opcode-selector specialisation of it, restricted to real rows. As in Picus~\citep{picus}, a propagation analysis derives every output column from the inputs as a sequence of steps (linear and inverse solving, bit and one-hot decoding, integer bounds, carry roots, and summaries of recurring sub-circuits such as a field operation with its carries or a byte comparison), with case splits on selector bits both witnesses share. The derivation is replayed in \Lean{} as a proof, one lemma per step, each lifted from $\F$ to the integers through the columns' ranges and closed by a decision procedure. The sub-circuit summaries are library lemmas proved once by hand, and a wide accelerator chip is proved as a chain of such lemmas.

\paragraph{Coverage.} The statements cover every core chip of Appendix~\ref{sec:app-chips} except the three preprocessed tables, which carry no statement, and the two SHA-256 control chips, which are not yet modelled: \ndettotal{} theorems over 57 chips. All are proved and checked by the \Lean{} kernel, with no axiom beyond the three standard ones. A change to a chip's constraints requires its statement to be extracted and checked again, and the recursion machine is not covered.

\paragraph{Findings.} A determinism statement is false when a row admits two satisfying witnesses with the same inputs and different outputs. For each statement the automation left open, we inspected the constraints for such a counterexample; Table~\ref{tab:det-findings} lists the outcome. Three were soundness gaps in the constraints, two exploitable and one latent, each closed by new constraints; the other three are prover freedom the protocol allows by design, which the statements now name as inputs.
