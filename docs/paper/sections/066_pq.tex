\subsection{Post-quantum security}
\label{sec:pq}

Every component of the shard and recursion arguments is hash-based: the Merkle commitments under $\Hash$, the \WHIR{} proximity test, the sumchecks and \LogUp{}, and the global challenge of \S\ref{sec:air-global}, a hash chain that seeds one more \LogUp{} challenge. The Fiat--Shamir compilation of such interactive oracle proofs remains sound against quantum adversaries in the quantum random-oracle model~\citep{cms19}, with weaker concrete bounds: a quantum collision search on the 248-bit digest of the measured instantiation costs about $2^{83}$ rather than $2^{124}$ evaluations~\citep{bht98}, and a quantum search gains quadratically against grinding, so the levels of \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness} are classical. The elliptic-curve multiset hash that the global challenge replaced was the exception: Shor's algorithm computes discrete logarithms in $E(\mathbb F_{p^7})$~\citep{shor97}, which reduces its soundness to finding a short integer relation among known scalars, and at the interaction counts of a block such a relation should be assumed easy to find. Of the other options of \S\ref{sec:air-global}, Merkle roots rest on hashes and a lattice multiset hash on a problem not known to be easy for quantum adversaries. The pairing-based wraps of \S\ref{sec:applications} are not post-quantum; the statement concerns the compressed proof. This paper computes no quantum security level, and so claims post-quantum security for no configuration.
