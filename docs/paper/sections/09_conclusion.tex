\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \Ziren{}, a succinct argument for MIPS32 execution. One row of one chip per executed instruction, carried by an instruction frame, replaces the CPU table; one lookup argument and one zerocheck per shard discharge every bus and constraint; a jagged reduction opened by \WHIR{} commits columns of any height at the cost of their real area; and a recursion tree with an enumerated key allowlist composes the shards into one proof. Conformance suites and an executable \Lean{} model test the emulator. Determinism is extracted from the constraint description the prover evaluates and proved by replaying a propagation analysis as step lemmas over gadget theorems proved once; all \ndettotal{} extracted theorems are closed in \Lean{}, the statements exposed three soundness gaps that the constraints now close, and the same pipeline extends to the remaining control chips and to the recursion machine.

Beyond determinism, two techniques surround the machine. Proving is pipelined: shards are proved as the emulator produces them, recursion nodes are proved while core shards are still in flight, and trace generation and the proof-of-work searches run on the device, so what remains serial is the start-up interval and the recursion tail (\S\ref{sec:perf-pipeline}). And the one component that is not hash-based, the cross-shard digest, has three hash-based or lattice-based replacements with measured or estimated costs: a global challenge, 29 to 42\% slower; Merkle roots over touched memory, faster than the digest on one GPU and 3.4\% slower on four; and a lattice multiset hash at no less than $19.6\times$ the committed area (\S\ref{sec:pq}).

The performance study identifies trace area, lookup interactions, layer order and shard size as distinct cost levers. One of the two largest costs it identifies is the cross-shard digest, 15\% of the block's area, which a digest of per-shard deltas or a larger shard would reduce. The open proof obligations are the five hypotheses of \S\ref{sec:security-relation}; future work should measure the corrected $\Poseidon{}$ schedule against one configuration, soundness report, key set and proof.
