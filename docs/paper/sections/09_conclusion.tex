\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \Ziren{}, a succinct argument for MIPS32 execution. One row of one chip per executed instruction, carried by an instruction frame, replaces the CPU table; one lookup argument, one zerocheck with a univariate first round and one jagged \WHIR{} opening prove each shard; and a recursion tree with an enumerated key allowlist composes the shards into one proof, which a native verifier checks and which terminal proofs wrap for on-chain verification, garbled verification or zero knowledge (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}, \S\ref{sec:zk}). Determinism is extracted from the constraint system itself: all \ndettotal{} extracted theorems are closed in \Lean{}, and stating them exposed three soundness gaps that the constraints now close.

The cost model expresses instruction efficiency in trace cells and bus interactions per guest instruction, separately from hardware-dependent proving throughput (\S\ref{sec:intro-costs}); frame overhead, shard-boundary memory rows and precompile expansion explain why guest-instruction counts alone do not measure prover work. The evaluation identifies the lookup argument and the cross-shard digest as the two largest device costs, and shows that once those are reduced the block time on two or more GPUs is bounded by host-side work per shard rather than by the devices (\S\ref{sec:perf-scaling}). The open proof obligations are the five hypotheses of \S\ref{sec:security-relation}.
