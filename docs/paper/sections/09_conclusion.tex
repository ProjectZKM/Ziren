\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \Ziren{}, a succinct argument for MIPS32 execution. One row of one chip per executed instruction, carried by an instruction frame, replaces the CPU table; one lookup argument, one zerocheck with a univariate first round and one jagged \WHIR{} opening prove each shard; and a recursion tree with an enumerated key allowlist composes the shards into one proof, which a native verifier checks and which terminal proofs wrap for on-chain verification, garbled verification or zero knowledge (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}, \S\ref{sec:zk}). Determinism is extracted from the constraint system itself: all \ndettotal{} extracted theorems are closed in \Lean{}, and stating them exposed three soundness gaps that the constraints now close.

The cost model expresses instruction efficiency in trace cells and bus interactions per guest instruction, separately from hardware-dependent proving throughput (\S\ref{sec:intro-costs}); frame overhead, shard-boundary memory rows and precompile expansion explain why guest-instruction counts alone do not measure prover work. The evaluation identifies the lookup argument and the rows of the elliptic-curve cross-shard digest as the two largest device costs, and shows that once those are reduced the block time on two or more GPUs is bounded by host-side work per shard rather than by the devices (\S\ref{sec:perf-scaling}). The global challenge removes the digest's rows and its computational assumption, and pays with small global proofs, each of which opens several rounds of one lane of its hash chain (\S\ref{sec:air-global}). With it, the degree-five extension and schedules solved for 128 bits per component, two GPUs prove 25 Ethereum blocks 5.1\,\% faster than the previous release did at about 100 bits (\S\ref{sec:perf-pq128}). The open proof obligations are the four hypotheses of \S\ref{sec:security-relation}.
