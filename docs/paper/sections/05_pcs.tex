\section{The jagged commitment}
\label{sec:pcs}

After the bus argument and the zerocheck of a shard (\S\S\ref{sec:air-logup} and~\ref{sec:air-zerocheck}), the verifier holds evaluation claims on individual chip columns, with heights from 32 rows to millions. Committing each column or table separately costs one Merkle root and one query phase per commitment, and puts every table's height into the verifier~\citep[p.~4]{jagged-pcs}. The jagged polynomial commitment of Hemo et al.~\citep{jagged-pcs} commits the non-zero part of all columns as one dense multilinear and reduces every column claim to one evaluation of it. \Ziren{} uses its basic construction with its jagged assist~\citep[Thms.~1.4 and~1.5]{jagged-pcs}, restated in \S\S\ref{sec:pcs-jagged-fn}--\ref{sec:pcs-jagged-assist}, and adds several ordered commitments opened as one and a quantised geometry that makes the verifier a function of a small class rather than of a shard's row counts (\S\S\ref{sec:pcs-stacking}--\ref{sec:pcs-geometry}). Figure~\ref{fig:jagged} shows the construction.

\subsection{Jagged functions and the dense representation}
\label{sec:pcs-jagged-fn}

Let there be $K$ columns $\mathbf c_0, \ldots, \mathbf c_{K-1}$ with heights $h_0, \ldots, h_{K-1} \le 2^{n}$, viewed as one function $p : \{0,1\}^n \times \{0,1\}^{\kappa} \to \F$, $\kappa = \lceil \log_2 K \rceil$, with $p(x, k) = \mathbf c_k[x]$ for $x < h_k$ and $p(x, k) = 0$ otherwise. Such a function is \emph{jagged}~\citep[Def.~1.1]{jagged-pcs}. Its non-zero part is the \emph{dense representation}
\[
  \mathbf Q = \mathbf c_0 \Vert \mathbf c_1 \Vert \cdots \Vert \mathbf c_{K-1},
  \qquad
  t_0 = 0,\quad t_{k+1} = t_k + h_k,\quad A = t_K,
\]
padded with zeros to $M = 2^m$, $m = \lceil \log_2 A \rceil$, and written $\widetilde Q$ as a multilinear in $m$ variables. Column $k$ occupies $[t_k, t_{k+1})$ in $\mathbf Q$, and the prefix sums are public: they enter the transcript before any challenge. $\mathbf Q$ and the prefix sums determine $p$~\citep[Fact~1.3]{jagged-pcs}, so committing to $\widetilde Q$ commits to every column at the cost of the real area $A$ rather than $K \cdot 2^n$. Our prefix sums are exclusive ($t_k$ starts column $k$), and a boundary takes $m+1$ bits because $t_K = A$ may equal $2^m$.

\subsection{The jagged reduction}
\label{sec:pcs-jagged}

Let $\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}\in\EF^n$ be the common row point left by the zerocheck, and $y_k$ the claimed evaluation of column $k$ there, high zeros included:
\[
  y_k = \sum_{j=0}^{h_k-1} Q[t_k+j]\,\eqf(j,\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}) = \widetilde p(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, k).
\]
The verifier samples a column point $\bm z_c\in\EF^\kappa$, which turns the $K$ claims into the single claim
\[
  T = \sum_{k<K} \eqf(k,\bm z_c)\, y_k = \widetilde p(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c)
\]
at an error of $\kappa/|\EF|$ by Schwartz--Zippel. With $w_k = \eqf(k, \bm z_c)$, the length-$M$ table
\[
  W[t_k+j]=w_k\,\eqf(j,\bm z_{\mathrm{row}})\quad(0\le j<h_k),
  \qquad W[i]=0\quad\text{elsewhere},
\]
is the indicator of Hemo et al.\ on the Boolean cube~\citep[Eqs.~(3)--(4)]{jagged-pcs}, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jagged-global-claim}
  T
  =\sum_kw_k\sum_{j<h_k}Q[t_k+j]\eqf(j,\bm z_{\mathrm{row}})
  =\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}^m}\widetilde Q(i)\widetilde W(i).
\end{equation}
$\widetilde W$ agrees with the product of equality polynomials only on the cube~\citep[Rem.~3.1]{jagged-pcs}, so the verifier cannot evaluate it by formula; \S\ref{sec:pcs-jagged-assist} supplies it.

\begin{theorem}[Basic jagged {\citep[Thm.~1.4]{jagged-pcs}}]
\label{thm:jagged}
There is an $m$-round protocol in which the prover holds $\mathbf Q$ and the verifier holds only the prefix sums, $(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c)$ and $T$, and at whose end the verifier either rejects or outputs a point $\bm z^{*} \in \EF^m$ and a value $v_Q$.
\emph{Completeness:} if $T = \widetilde p(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c)$ then $v_Q = \widetilde Q(\bm z^{*})$.
\emph{Soundness:} if $T \neq \widetilde p(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c)$, then except with probability $2m/|\EF|$ the verifier rejects or $v_Q \neq \widetilde Q(\bm z^{*})$.
\emph{Efficiency:} the prover performs at most $5 \cdot 2^m + 2^n + K$ multiplications; the verifier's work is an arithmetic circuit determined by $m$ and $K$ alone, dominated by one evaluation of $\widetilde W(\bm z^{*})$.
\end{theorem}

The protocol is the sumcheck for a product of two multilinears~\citep[Lemma~2.3]{jagged-pcs} applied to Equation~\eqref{eq:jagged-global-claim}, the \emph{jagged sumcheck}, with round polynomials of degree two. With challenges $\bm z^*=(r_0,\ldots,r_{m-1})$ in sampling order, least-significant variable first, the closing claim is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jagged-closing}
  T_{\mathrm{final}}=\widetilde Q(\bm z^*)\,\widetilde W(\bm z^*).
\end{equation}
The proof carries $v_Q=\widetilde Q(\bm z^*)$, which the opening of \S\ref{sec:pcs-multiround-open} authenticates; the verifier obtains $\widetilde W(\bm z^*)$ from the public prefix sums.

\subsection{Evaluating the indicator: the jagged assist}
\label{sec:pcs-jagged-assist}

Materialising $W$ would cost $\Theta(M)$ extension-field elements. Instead, the indicator is a sum over columns of a function of each column's boundaries~\citep[Claim~3.2.1, Eq.~(5)]{jagged-pcs},
\[
  \widetilde W(\bm z^{*}) = \sum_{k<K} w_k\, \widehat g(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z^{*}, t_k, t_{k+1}),
  \qquad g(a, b, c, d) = 1 \iff b < d \ \wedge\ b = a + c,
\]
where $g$ is computed by a width-four read-once branching program that reads one bit of each of $(a, b, c, d)$ at a time, least significant first, tracking the carry of $a + c$ and whether $b < d$~\citep[Claim~3.2.2]{jagged-pcs}. Its multilinear extension is evaluated by the dynamic program of Holmgren and Rothblum~\citep{hr18}, as in Hemo et al.~\citep[Lemma~4.2]{jagged-pcs}; the program takes points most-significant coordinate first while the sumcheck samples least-significant first, so its point is $\operatorname{rev}(\bm z^*)$. Evaluated directly, the indicator costs $O(m K)$~\citep[Prop.~3.2]{jagged-pcs}, too much for a recursion circuit at $K \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^4$.

The \emph{jagged assist}~\citep[Thm.~1.5]{jagged-pcs} delegates it. With $u_k=\operatorname{bits}_{\mathrm{BE}}(t_k)\Vert\operatorname{bits}_{\mathrm{BE}}(t_{k+1})$ the $2(m+1)$ boundary bits of column $k$, the prover proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jagged-assist}
  \widetilde W(\bm z^*)
  =\sum_{x,y\in\{0,1\}^{m+1}}
    \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}
      w_k\,
      \eqf((x,y),u_k)\,
      \mathsf{BP}(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}},\operatorname{rev}(\bm z^*),x,y)
\end{equation}
by one degree-two sumcheck over the $2(m+1)$ boundary variables. This is the batch evaluation protocol of Hemo et al.~\citep[Lemma~5.1]{jagged-pcs} specialised: the $K$ evaluations are already combined by the weights $w_k$, so no further randomness is needed and the error is that of the sumcheck alone.

\begin{theorem}[Jagged assist {\citep[Thm.~1.5, Lemma~5.1]{jagged-pcs}}, as instantiated]
\label{thm:jagged-assist}
\emph{Completeness:} if $\widetilde W(\bm z^*)$ is claimed correctly, the verifier accepts.
\emph{Soundness:} otherwise it rejects except with probability $4(m+1)/|\EF|$.
\emph{Efficiency:} the verifier evaluates $\mathsf{BP}$ once, $48(m+1)$ multiplications, and the $K$ equality factors $\sum_k w_k\,\eqf(\bm\rho, u_k)$ at the closing point $\bm\rho$, $O(mK)$ operations; the prover's cost is $O(m w^2 (K + w))$ operations for a program of width $w$~\citep[Lemma~4.6]{jagged-pcs}.
\end{theorem}

The second verifier term dominates: about $2 \cdot 10^6$ extension multiplications at the core shard's $m$ and $K$, against a few thousand for the branching program.

\subsection{Row weights after the univariate skip}
\label{sec:pcs-skip}

For the refinement of \S\ref{sec:air-skip}, each row factor $\eqf(j,\bm z_{\mathrm{row}})$ in the column claims and in Equation~\eqref{eq:jagged-global-claim} is replaced by the weight of Equation~\eqref{eq:skip-row-opening},
\[
  \psi(j)=\eqf(\bm z_{\mathrm{hi}},j\mathbin{\gg}4)
           L_{j\bmod16}(r_0),\qquad
  W^\sharp[t_k+j]=w_k\psi(j)\quad(0\le j<h_k).
\]
The implicit dense tail still has zero weight, and the product sumcheck on $\langle\widetilde Q,\widetilde W^\sharp\rangle$ remains degree two, since the row weights determine a Boolean table whose extension in the dense index is multilinear. The assist uses the same boundaries and, with $\bm b$ the four-bit encoding of $b$, the boundary polynomial
\[
  \mathsf{BP}^\sharp(r_0,\bm z_{\mathrm{hi}},\bm z^*,x,y)
    =\sum_{b=0}^{15}L_b(r_0)\,
       \mathsf{BP}((\bm z_{\mathrm{hi}},\bm b),
                  \operatorname{rev}(\bm z^*),x,y),
\]
in which the high row bits follow the ordinary dynamic program and the four low row bits are enumerated over Boolean values and combined with $L_b(r_0)$. This polynomial is still multilinear in the boundary inputs, so the degree-two assist and its $4(m+1)/|\EF|$ sumcheck term are unchanged, although the branching-program evaluation has additional constant-size work. The dense commitment, prefix sums, stacking layout and final \WHIR{} opening are unchanged.

\subsection{Several commitments opened as one}
\label{sec:pcs-stacking}

The construction of Hemo et al.~\citep{jagged-pcs} assumes one dense oracle. \Ziren{} commits a shard in several ordered \emph{input-commitment rounds}, preprocessed first and main last, each with its own root, and opens them together; each round uses the stacked layout of SP1 Hypercube~\citep{sp1-hypercube}.

Round $r$ contains columns $\mathbf c_{r,0},\ldots,\mathbf c_{r,K_r-1}$ of real area $a_r = \sum_k h_{r,k}$, committed over an area $A_r \ge a_r$ that is a multiple of the stacking height $H=2^\ell$:
\[
  \mathbf q_r = \mathbf c_{r,0}\Vert\cdots\Vert\mathbf c_{r,K_r-1}
                 \Vert \mathbf 0^{A_r-a_r},
  \qquad
  \mathbf Q = \mathbf q_0\Vert\mathbf q_1\Vert\cdots\Vert\mathbf q_{R-1},
  \qquad A=\sum_{r<R}A_r.
\]
The reduction of \S\ref{sec:pcs-jagged} runs on this logical $\mathbf Q$, and the Merkle roots $C_0,\ldots,C_{R-1}$ authenticate the input rounds separately. Each gap $A_r-a_r$ is represented by explicit synthetic zero columns of zero claim and height at most $2^n$, so the prefix sums describe every committed cell and only the suffix from $A$ to $M$ is implicit; on that suffix the indicator vanishes because $g$ requires $b<d$~\citep[fn.~4]{jagged-pcs}.

Each round is cut into $S_r=A_r/H$ stripes of $H$ consecutive entries under its own root. With $S=\sum_r S_r$ and $b=\lceil\log_2\max(S,1)\rceil$, the opening domain has $\ell+b$ variables, which equals $m$ for this layout. Its point is $(\bm z_{\mathrm{stack}},\bm z_{\mathrm{batch}})$: $\ell$ coordinates select a position inside a stripe and $b$ interpolate the ordered list of stripes across all rounds. Were the reduction point shorter than $\ell+b$ coordinates, further coordinates would be sampled after the assist, each contributing a factor $1-r_j$ (step~4 of Protocol~\ref{prot:jagged}); in this layout none are needed.

\subsection{One opening across all roots}
\label{sec:pcs-multiround-open}

For round $r$ and stripe $s$ let $e_{r,s}=\widetilde{\mathbf q_{r,s}}(\bm z_{\mathrm{stack}})$. The prover reports these in round-major, stripe-major order; the verifier zero-pads the list to $2^b$ and checks
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stacking-bind}
  v_Q
  =\widetilde{(e_{0,0},\ldots,e_{0,S_0-1},e_{1,0},\ldots,e_{R-1,S_{R-1}-1})}
     (\bm z_{\mathrm{batch}}).
\end{equation}
One stacked \WHIR{} proof then authenticates every $e_{r,s}$ against $C_0,\ldots,C_{R-1}$ at the common stack point, so the reduction, assist and opening each run once while each input round retains its own Merkle root; all rounds must share the stacking height. Protocol~\ref{prot:jagged} collects the steps.

\paragraph{Soundness.} The three algebraic steps contribute $\kappa/|\EF|$ (column batching), $2m/|\EF|$ (jagged sumcheck, Theorem~\ref{thm:jagged}) and $4(m+1)/|\EF|$ (assist, Theorem~\ref{thm:jagged-assist}), which together form the row ``reduction to the dense polynomial'' of Table~\ref{tab:components}. Hemo et al.\ state plain soundness of each reduction; the round-by-round form Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} assumes follows because each is a sumcheck, round-by-round sound with the same per-round errors~\citep{chiesa-yogev}. The opening's terms are those of Section~\ref{sec:iopp}, and all of them are accounted in \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness}.

\subsection{Quantised committed geometry}
\label{sec:pcs-geometry}

The stacking height is $H=2^{21}$, so a round is committed as a whole number of $2^{21}$-cell blocks, and a column is at most $2^{22}$ rows, which bounds the gap one synthetic column can absorb. A round with $\nu=\lceil a_r/H\rceil$ blocks of real data is committed in a \emph{bucket} of $\hat\nu$ blocks, where $\hat\nu=\nu$ for $\nu\le4$ and otherwise the next multiple of eight:
\[
  1,\;2,\;3,\;4,\;8,\;16,\;24,\;\ldots\ \text{blocks}.
\]
For a core round the gap is split into
\[
  \Bigl\lceil \frac{(\hat\nu-\hat\nu_{\mathrm{prev}})\,H}{2^{22}} \Bigr\rceil
  \ \text{columns},\qquad \hat\nu_{\mathrm{prev}}=\text{the bucket below }\hat\nu,
\]
at least one: the number the widest gap of the bucket needs, so the count depends on the bucket alone, and a narrower gap leaves trailing columns short or empty. A round whose area is pinned, as in the recursion machine, uses a fixed number of columns instead, zero-height ones allowed, so the recursion program's column count does not depend on a child's row counts.

Both rules make a shard's committed geometry, and hence the recursion program that verifies it and that program's key, a function of the chip set, the first-shard flag and the two buckets rather than of exact row counts. Without the quantisation every row profile would be a distinct verifier program; with it the key space is small enough to enumerate in advance, which lets a verifier hold a fixed allowlist covering every shard shape the declared clusters admit (Section~\ref{sec:argument-block}). The overhead is the padding inside a bucket, committed and opened like any other cell.
