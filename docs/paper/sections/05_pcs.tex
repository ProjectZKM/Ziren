\section{The jagged commitment}
\label{sec:pcs}

After the bus argument and the zerocheck of a shard (\S\S\ref{sec:air-logup} and~\ref{sec:air-zerocheck}), the verifier holds evaluation claims on individual chip columns: about $3.6 \cdot 10^4$ per \texttt{reth} shard, with heights from 32 rows to millions. Committing each column or table separately costs one Merkle root and one query phase per commitment, and puts every table's height into the verifier, so that each height profile needs a verifier circuit of its own~\citep[p.~4]{jagged-pcs}. The jagged polynomial commitment of Hemo et al.~\citep{jagged-pcs} commits the non-zero part of all columns as one dense multilinear and reduces every column claim to one evaluation of it. \Ziren{} uses its basic construction~\citep[Thm.~1.4]{jagged-pcs} with its jagged assist~\citep[Thm.~1.5]{jagged-pcs}; it uses neither the ``fancy'' variant for columns of equal height (\S6 there) nor committed heights proved by GKR (\S1.1.2 there), because the machine's columns are too heterogeneous for grouping into power-of-two tables to pay.

Sections~\ref{sec:pcs-jagged-fn}--\ref{sec:pcs-jagged-assist} restate the construction in our notation. Sections~\ref{sec:pcs-stacking}--\ref{sec:pcs-geometry} describe what \Ziren{} adds: several ordered commitments opened as one, the stacked layout of the opening, and a quantised geometry that makes the verifier a function of a small class rather than of a shard's row counts. A chip can therefore be widened, added or removed without changing the layer above it; the chip revisions of \S\ref{sec:perf-changes} left this protocol unchanged. Figure~\ref{fig:jagged} shows the construction end to end.

\subsection{Jagged functions and the dense representation}
\label{sec:pcs-jagged-fn}

Let there be $K$ columns $\mathbf c_0, \ldots, \mathbf c_{K-1}$ with heights $h_0, \ldots, h_{K-1} \le 2^{n}$, viewed as one function $p : \{0,1\}^n \times \{0,1\}^{\kappa} \to \F$, $\kappa = \lceil \log_2 K \rceil$, with $p(x, k) = \mathbf c_k[x]$ for $x < h_k$ and $p(x, k) = 0$ otherwise. Such a function is \emph{jagged}~\citep[Def.~1.1]{jagged-pcs}. Its non-zero part is the \emph{dense representation}
\[
  \mathbf Q = \mathbf c_0 \Vert \mathbf c_1 \Vert \cdots \Vert \mathbf c_{K-1},
  \qquad
  t_0 = 0,\quad t_{k+1} = t_k + h_k,\quad A = t_K,
\]
padded with zeros to $M = 2^m$, $m = \lceil \log_2 A \rceil$, and written $\widetilde Q$ as a multilinear in $m$ variables. Column $k$ occupies $[t_k, t_{k+1})$ in $\mathbf Q$, and the prefix sums are public: they enter the transcript before any challenge. $\mathbf Q$ and the prefix sums determine $p$~\citep[Fact~1.3]{jagged-pcs}, so committing to $\widetilde Q$ commits to every column at the cost of the real area $A$ rather than $K \cdot 2^n$.

Table~\ref{tab:jagged-notation} maps our notation to the paper's. Two differences are inessential: our prefix sums are exclusive ($t_k$ starts column $k$), and a boundary takes $m+1$ bits because $t_K = A$ may equal $2^m$. Heights may reach $2^n$ rather than $2^n - 1$, which is harmless because only boundaries are encoded.

\subsection{The jagged reduction}
\label{sec:pcs-jagged}

Let $\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}\in\EF^n$ be the common row point left by the zerocheck, and $y_k$ the claimed evaluation of column $k$ there, high zeros included:
\[
  y_k = \sum_{j=0}^{h_k-1} Q[t_k+j]\,\eqf(j,\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}) = \widetilde p(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, k).
\]
The verifier samples a column point $\bm z_c\in\EF^\kappa$, which turns the $K$ claims into the single claim
\[
  T = \sum_{k<K} \eqf(k,\bm z_c)\, y_k = \widetilde p(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c)
\]
at an error of $\kappa/|\EF|$ by Schwartz--Zippel; this is the input of the basic jagged construction. With $w_k = \eqf(k, \bm z_c)$, the length-$M$ table
\[
  W[t_k+j]=w_k\,\eqf(j,\bm z_{\mathrm{row}})\quad(0\le j<h_k),
  \qquad W[i]=0\quad\text{elsewhere},
\]
is the paper's indicator $f_t(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c, \cdot)$ on the Boolean cube~\citep[Eqs.~(3)--(4)]{jagged-pcs}, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jagged-global-claim}
  T
  =\sum_kw_k\sum_{j<h_k}Q[t_k+j]\eqf(j,\bm z_{\mathrm{row}})
  =\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}^m}\widetilde Q(i)\widetilde W(i).
\end{equation}
$\widetilde W$ agrees with the product of equality polynomials only on the cube~\citep[Rem.~3.1]{jagged-pcs}, so the verifier cannot evaluate it by formula; \S\ref{sec:pcs-jagged-assist} supplies it.

\begin{theorem}[Basic jagged {\citep[Thm.~1.4]{jagged-pcs}}]
\label{thm:jagged}
There is an $m$-round protocol in which the prover holds $\mathbf Q$ and the verifier holds only the prefix sums, $(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c)$ and $T$, and at whose end the verifier either rejects or outputs a point $\bm z^{*} \in \EF^m$ and a value $v_Q$.
\emph{Completeness:} if $T = \widetilde p(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c)$ then $v_Q = \widetilde Q(\bm z^{*})$.
\emph{Soundness:} if $T \neq \widetilde p(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z_c)$, then except with probability $2m/|\EF|$ the verifier rejects or $v_Q \neq \widetilde Q(\bm z^{*})$.
\emph{Efficiency:} the prover performs at most $5 \cdot 2^m + 2^n + K$ multiplications; the verifier's work is an arithmetic circuit determined by $m$ and $K$ alone, dominated by one evaluation of $\widetilde W(\bm z^{*})$.
\end{theorem}

The protocol is the sumcheck for a product of two multilinears~\citep[Lemma~2.3]{jagged-pcs} applied to Equation~\eqref{eq:jagged-global-claim}, the \emph{jagged sumcheck}. Each round polynomial has degree two and is sent as its values at $0,1,2$, one value more than the paper's accounting~\citep[App.~A]{jagged-pcs}. With challenges $\bm z^*=(r_0,\ldots,r_{m-1})$ in sampling order, least-significant variable first, the closing claim is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jagged-closing}
  T_{\mathrm{final}}=\widetilde Q(\bm z^*)\,\widetilde W(\bm z^*).
\end{equation}
The proof carries $v_Q=\widetilde Q(\bm z^*)$, which the opening of \S\ref{sec:pcs-multiround-open} authenticates; the verifier obtains $\widetilde W(\bm z^*)$ from the public prefix sums.

\subsection{Evaluating the indicator: the jagged assist}
\label{sec:pcs-jagged-assist}

Materialising $W$ would cost $\Theta(M)$ extension-field elements. Instead, the indicator is a sum over columns of a function of each column's boundaries~\citep[Claim~3.2.1, Eq.~(5)]{jagged-pcs},
\[
  \widetilde W(\bm z^{*}) = \sum_{k<K} w_k\, \widehat g(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}}, \bm z^{*}, t_k, t_{k+1}),
  \qquad g(a, b, c, d) = 1 \iff b < d \ \wedge\ b = a + c,
\]
where $g$ is computed by a width-four read-once branching program that reads one bit of each of $(a, b, c, d)$ at a time, least significant first, tracking the carry of $a + c$ and whether $b < d$~\citep[Claim~3.2.2]{jagged-pcs}. Its multilinear extension is evaluated by the dynamic program of Holmgren and Rothblum~\citep{hr18}, stated and proved as in Hemo et al.~\citep[Lemma~4.2]{jagged-pcs}: $48(m+1)$ multiplications per point at our width and alphabet. Points are passed to the program most-significant coordinate first~\citep[p.~5]{jagged-pcs} while the sumcheck samples least-significant first, so the program's point is $\operatorname{rev}(\bm z^*)$. Evaluated directly, the indicator costs $O(m K)$~\citep[Prop.~3.2]{jagged-pcs}, too much for a recursion circuit at $K \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^4$.

The \emph{jagged assist}~\citep[Thm.~1.5]{jagged-pcs} delegates it. With $u_k=\operatorname{bits}_{\mathrm{BE}}(t_k)\Vert\operatorname{bits}_{\mathrm{BE}}(t_{k+1})$ the $2(m+1)$ boundary bits of column $k$, the prover proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jagged-assist}
  \widetilde W(\bm z^*)
  =\sum_{x,y\in\{0,1\}^{m+1}}
    \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}
      w_k\,
      \eqf((x,y),u_k)\,
      \mathsf{BP}(\bm z_{\mathrm{row}},\operatorname{rev}(\bm z^*),x,y)
\end{equation}
by one degree-two sumcheck over the $2(m+1)$ boundary variables of Equation~\eqref{eq:jagged-assist}. This is the batch evaluation protocol of Hemo et al.~\citep[Lemma~5.1]{jagged-pcs} specialised: the $K$ evaluations are already combined by the weights $w_k$ rather than by fresh random coefficients, so no further randomness is needed and the error is that of the sumcheck alone, $4(m+1)/|\EF|$.

\begin{theorem}[Jagged assist {\citep[Thm.~1.5, Lemma~5.1]{jagged-pcs}}, as instantiated]
\label{thm:jagged-assist}
\emph{Completeness:} if $\widetilde W(\bm z^*)$ is claimed correctly, the verifier accepts.
\emph{Soundness:} otherwise it rejects except with probability $4(m+1)/|\EF|$.
\emph{Efficiency:} the verifier evaluates $\mathsf{BP}$ once, $48(m+1)$ multiplications, and the $K$ equality factors $\sum_k w_k\,\eqf(\bm\rho, u_k)$ at the closing point $\bm\rho$, $O(mK)$ operations; the prover's cost is $O(m w^2 (K + w))$ operations for a program of width $w$~\citep[Lemma~4.6]{jagged-pcs}.
\end{theorem}

The second verifier term dominates: it is linear in the number of columns, about $2 \cdot 10^6$ extension multiplications at the core shard's $m$ and $K$, against a few thousand for the branching program, consistent with the paper's $m \cdot 2^k$~\citep[p.~7]{jagged-pcs}. The native verifier computes the value by the closed form and replays the assist transcript; the recursion circuit checks the assist's closing identity explicitly.

\subsection{Several commitments opened as one}
\label{sec:pcs-stacking}

The paper assumes one dense oracle. \Ziren{} commits a shard in several ordered \emph{input-commitment rounds}, preprocessed first and main last, each with its own root, and opens them together; each round uses the stacked layout of SP1 Hypercube~\citep{sp1-hypercube}, which the paper's concrete evaluation also uses, cutting the dense side into $2^8$ blocks of $2^{21}$ cells~\citep[\S7]{jagged-pcs}.

Round $r$ contains columns $\mathbf c_{r,0},\ldots,\mathbf c_{r,K_r-1}$ of real area $a_r = \sum_k h_{r,k}$, committed over an area $A_r \ge a_r$ that is a multiple of the stacking height $H=2^\ell$:
\[
  \mathbf q_r = \mathbf c_{r,0}\Vert\cdots\Vert\mathbf c_{r,K_r-1}
                 \Vert \mathbf 0^{A_r-a_r},
  \qquad
  \mathbf Q = \mathbf q_0\Vert\mathbf q_1\Vert\cdots\Vert\mathbf q_{R-1},
  \qquad A=\sum_{r<R}A_r.
\]
The reduction of \S\ref{sec:pcs-jagged} runs on this logical $\mathbf Q$; the roots $C_0,\ldots,C_{R-1}$ remain separate and their order is load-bearing. Each gap $A_r-a_r$ is represented by explicit synthetic zero columns of zero claim and height at most $2^n$, so the prefix sums describe every committed cell and only the suffix from $A$ to $M$ is implicit. That suffix is the paper's ``free padding''~\citep[fn.~4]{jagged-pcs}, on which the indicator vanishes because $g$ requires $b<d$. Under a recursion area pin the gap is split into a fixed number of columns, zero-height ones allowed, so the recursion program's column count does not depend on a child's row counts.

Each round is cut into $S_r=A_r/H$ stripes of $H$ consecutive entries under its own root. With $S=\sum_r S_r$ and $b=\lceil\log_2\max(S,1)\rceil$, the opening domain has $\ell+b$ variables, which for a nonempty ordinary instance equals $m$. Its point is $(\bm z_{\mathrm{stack}},\bm z_{\mathrm{batch}})$: $\ell$ coordinates select a position inside a stripe and $b$ interpolate the ordered list of stripes across all rounds.

\subsection{One opening across all roots}
\label{sec:pcs-multiround-open}

If the reduction point has fewer than $\ell+b$ coordinates, the prover appends Fiat--Shamir coordinates after the assist, each contributing a factor $1-r_j$; in the ordinary multi-round layout $m=\ell+b$ and this is a no-op. For round $r$ and stripe $s$ let $e_{r,s}=\widetilde{\mathbf q_{r,s}}(\bm z_{\mathrm{stack}})$. The prover reports these in round-major, stripe-major order; the verifier zero-pads the list to $2^b$ and checks
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stacking-bind}
  v_Q
  =\widetilde{(e_{0,0},\ldots,e_{0,S_0-1},e_{1,0},\ldots,e_{R-1,S_{R-1}-1})}
     (\bm z_{\mathrm{batch}}).
\end{equation}
One stacked \WHIR{} or \BaseFold{} proof then authenticates every $e_{r,s}$ against $C_0,\ldots,C_{R-1}$ at the common stack point, so the reduction, assist and opening each run once while commitments stay per round. All rounds must share the stacking height and PCS mode; a mixed transcript is rejected. The implementation also offers BaseFold as the opening; every configuration in this paper uses \WHIR{}.

The step order, the round order and the reversal convention fix the challenges; changing any of them is a protocol change.

\paragraph{Soundness.} The three algebraic steps contribute $\kappa/|\EF|$ (column batching), $2m/|\EF|$ (jagged sumcheck, Theorem~\ref{thm:jagged}) and $4(m+1)/|\EF|$ (assist, Theorem~\ref{thm:jagged-assist}), which together form the row ``reduction to the dense polynomial'' of Table~\ref{tab:components}. The paper states plain soundness of each reduction; the round-by-round form Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} assumes follows because each is a sumcheck, round-by-round sound with the same per-round errors~\citep{chiesa-yogev}. The opening's terms are those of Section~\ref{sec:iopp}, and all of them are accounted in \S\ref{sec:iopp-soundness}.

\subsection{Quantised committed geometry}
\label{sec:pcs-geometry}

The stacking height is $H=2^{21}$, so a round is committed as a whole number of $2^{21}$-cell blocks, and a column is at most $2^{22}$ rows, which bounds the gap one synthetic column can absorb. A round with $\nu=\lceil a_r/H\rceil$ blocks of real data is committed in a \emph{bucket} of $\hat\nu$ blocks, where $\hat\nu=\nu$ for $\nu\le4$ and otherwise the next multiple of eight:
\[
  1,\;2,\;3,\;4,\;8,\;16,\;24,\;\ldots\ \text{blocks}.
\]
For an unpinned round, which is every core round, the gap is split into
\[
  \Bigl\lceil \frac{(\hat\nu-\hat\nu_{\mathrm{prev}})\,H}{2^{22}} \Bigr\rceil
  \ \text{columns},\qquad \hat\nu_{\mathrm{prev}}=\text{the bucket below }\hat\nu,
\]
at least one: the number the widest gap of the bucket needs, so the count depends on the bucket alone, and a narrower gap leaves trailing columns short or empty. A pinned round, as in the compress machine, uses a fixed column count.

Both rules make a shard's committed geometry, and hence the recursion program that verifies it and that program's key, a function of the chip set, the first-shard flag and the two buckets rather than of exact row counts. Rounding up to the next block, or letting the padding-column count follow the gap, would make every row profile a distinct verifier program. Under the quantised rule the key space is small enough to enumerate offline, one representative per (chip set, first-shard flag, preprocessed bucket, main bucket) class, which lets a verifier hold a fixed allowlist covering every shard shape the declared clusters admit (Section~\ref{sec:recursion}). The price is the padding inside a bucket, committed and opened like any other cell.

The reduction's memory stays proportional to the real data: $W$ is never materialised (\S\ref{sec:pcs-jagged-assist}); the suffix from $A$ to $M$ is treated as zero rather than allocated, which is why inter-round padding must be explicit and the suffix need not be; and the stripe evaluations of Equation~\eqref{eq:stacking-bind} are read off the committed interleaved layout, so a round is traversed once to commit and once to open.
