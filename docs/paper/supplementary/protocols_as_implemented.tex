\section{Protocol details}
\label{sec:app-protocols}

\subsection{The \WHIR{} iteration as implemented}
\label{sec:app-whir}

Section~\ref{sec:iopp} states what one iteration does; this appendix gives it step by step. An \emph{iteration} with folding factor $k$ reduces membership in $\CRS[\F, L, m, \widehat w, \sigma]$ to membership of a new function $f'$ in $\CRS[\F, L^{(2)}, m - k, \widehat w', \sigma']$: the number of variables drops by $k$ while the domain only halves, so the code's rate falls from $\rho$ to $2^{1-k}\rho$ (its redundancy $1/\rho$ grows) in the paper's convention; \Zirenver{} instead re-encodes the folded polynomial at an explicit per-round rate (below). An iteration proceeds as follows~\citep[\S2.1.3]{whir}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Sumcheck rounds.} $k$ rounds of sumcheck on $\sum_{\bm b} \widehat w(\widehat f(\bm b), \bm b) = \sigma$, producing challenges $\bm\alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_k)$ and reducing the claim to one about $\widehat f(\bm\alpha, \cdot)$.
  \item \textbf{Folded oracle.} The prover sends $g : L^{(2)} \to \F$, honestly the evaluation of $\widehat g = \widehat f(\bm\alpha, \cdot)$, re-encoded at the round's rate.
  \item \textbf{Out-of-domain samples.} The verifier draws $z_0 \in \EF$ (two such points in \Zirenver{}); the prover answers $y_0 = \widehat g(\bm z_0)$ with $\bm z_0 = (z_0, z_0^2, \dots)$.
  \item \textbf{Shift queries.} The verifier draws $t$ indices $z_1, \dots, z_t \in L^{(2^k)}$, obtains $y_i = \Fold(f, \bm\alpha)(z_i)$ by reading the $2^k$ symbols of $f$ that fold to $z_i$ (one Merkle leaf each), and draws a combination scalar $\gamma$. As with the out-of-domain point, $\bm z_i = (z_i, z_i^2, \dots)$ denotes the power vector of $z_i$.
  \item \textbf{Recursive claim.} The new weight is $\widehat w'(Z, \bm X) = \widehat w(Z, \bm\alpha, \bm X) + Z \sum_i \gamma^{i+1} \eqf(\bm z_i, \bm X)$ and the new target $\sigma' = \sigma_k + \sum_{i=0}^{t} \gamma^{i+1} y_i$, where $\sigma_k$ is the value the round's $k$ sumcheck steps leave and the sum runs over the out-of-domain answer $y_0$ and the $t$ shift answers; the protocol recurses on $(g, \widehat w', \sigma')$.
\end{enumerate}
After the last iteration the prover sends the coefficients of the final polynomial in the clear and the verifier checks the residual constraint directly.

\subsection{The multi-round jagged reduction and assist}
\label{sec:app-jagged}

\paragraph{Multi-round layout and reduction.} Input-commitment rounds are
ordered with the preprocessed round first and the main round last. Round $r$
has real area $a_r$, committed area $A_r$, and root $C_r$. The implementation
inserts synthetic zero columns covering every cell in $A_r-a_r$, then
concatenates the committed areas into one logical vector $\mathbf Q$ of length
$A=\sum_r A_r$. Thus the global prefix sums include the padding between roots
and end at $t_K=A$, although the roots themselves remain separate.

After the surrounding protocol has absorbed the roots and column claims, the
verifier samples $\bm z_c$ and sets $\lambda_k=\eqf(k,\bm z_c)$. For the
common zerocheck row point $\bm z_r$, the dense weight table is
\[
  W[t_k+j]=\lambda_k\eqf(j,\bm z_r)
  \quad(0\le j<t_{k+1}-t_k),
  \qquad W[i]=0\quad\text{otherwise}.
\]
The implementation runs the degree-two sumcheck
\[
  \sum_k\lambda_k y_k
  =\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}^m}\widetilde Q(i)\widetilde W(i),
  \qquad m=\lceil\log_2 A\rceil,
\]
transmits each round polynomial as its evaluations at $0,1,2$, and binds the
least-significant variable first. Its sampled point $\bm z^*$ is therefore
stored in sample order, and the closing identity is
$T_{\mathrm{final}}=v_Q\widetilde W(\bm z^*)$ with
$v_Q=\widetilde Q(\bm z^*)$.

\paragraph{Evaluating $\widetilde f_t$: the branching program.} Proposition~3.2 of~\citep{jagged-pcs} writes
\[
  \widetilde f_t(\bm z_r, \bm z_c, i) = \sum_{y \in \{0,1\}^{k}} \eqf(\bm z_c, y)\, \widehat g(\bm z_r, i, t_{y}, t_{y+1}),
\]
where $g(a, b, c, d) = 1$ iff $b < d$ and $b = a + c$ as integers. The function $g$ is computed by a width-four read-once branching program reading one bit of each of $a, b, c, d$ per step (two bits of state: the carry of $a + c$ and whether the prefix of $b$ is below the prefix of $d$), so by Holmgren--Rothblum~\citep{hr18} its multilinear extension is evaluated in $O(m)$ field operations by a dynamic program over the layers. \Zirenver{} implements exactly this program: four memory states, four bits read per layer (sixteen bit-states), evaluated in reverse over $m + 1$ layers. The verifier does not sum the $2^k$ columns directly because $K \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^4$. Instead, the prover supplies the ``jagged assist'' of Section~5 of the paper and proves
\[
  \mathsf{jagged\_eval} = \sum_{x, y \in \{0,1\}^{m+1}} \sum_{k} \mathsf{L}_k(\bm z_c)\; \eqf\big((x, y), (\mathrm{bits}_{\mathrm{BE}}(t_k), \mathrm{bits}_{\mathrm{BE}}(t_{k+1}))\big)\; \mathsf{BP}(\bm z_r, \operatorname{rev}(\bm z^{*}), x, y)
\]
by a $2(m+1)$-variable sumcheck, where $\mathsf L_k$ is the $k$-th Lagrange weight of $\bm z_c$ and $\mathsf{BP}$ the branching-program polynomial. The reversal is required because the reduction binds least-significant variables first while the branching program reads integer indices most-significant bit first. The verifier finishes with one evaluation of $\mathsf{BP}$ at the sumcheck point and an in-the-clear check of the closing identity against the public prefix sums.

\paragraph{Stacked opening and transcript.} Let $H=2^\ell$ and $S=\sum_r A_r/H$. The opening point has $\ell+\lceil\log_2\max(S,1)\rceil$ coordinates: its first $\ell$ coordinates evaluate inside every stripe and the remainder interpolate the flat, round-major list of stripe evaluations, padded with zeros. If necessary, coordinates extending $\bm z^*$ are sampled after the assist. One stacked \WHIR{} or \BaseFold{} proof authenticates that list against $C_0,\ldots,C_{R-1}$; all input rounds use the same stacking height and PCS mode. The exact Fiat--Shamir order is $\bm z_c$, Hadamard sumcheck, assist sumcheck, point extension, then batched PCS opening.
